Where It Starts

A waste yard in Kishangarh

Rajasthan's marble mines leave behind a fine white slurry — a truck arrives roughly every ten minutes just to dump it. It's been treated as a pollution problem for years. We turn it into paper instead.

What Makes It Different

No trees. No water. Still paper.

Tree-free

Made from ground marble waste, not wood pulp — no forest goes into this notebook.

Water-free

Regular paper uses roughly 200 litres of water per kilogram made. Ours uses none in the sheet-making process.

Tougher

Naturally tear- and water-resistant — it survives a spilled cup of chai that would ruin a normal notebook.

Being Honest With You

What we claim, and what we don't

What's true

  • No trees cut for this paper
  • No water used in making the sheet
  • Far less energy than standard papermaking

What we won't claim

Our paper contains a small amount of HDPE (plastic) as a binder — about 18%. That means it isn't compostable and isn't "plastic-free." It breaks down under sunlight over time, but not biodegradably. We'd rather tell you this upfront than have you assume otherwise.

Behind the Scenes

Made close to where the stone comes from

Local, not imported

We're building our plant near Kishangarh — close enough to the marble belt that the raw material barely travels before it becomes paper.

A family business

Two founders and a senior family member with hands-on carton-industry experience, building this from the ground up — not a corporate sustainability initiative, a real small manufacturer.

A Note From Us

Why we're doing this

We grew up around Rajasthan's marble industry and watched its waste pile up as a problem nobody wanted to solve. Paper felt like the most useful thing we could turn it into — something people already use every day, made a little differently.
