## 1. Executive Summary

"Stone paper" (also sold as mineral paper or rich mineral paper) replaces wood pulp with ~80% ground calcium carbonate (limestone/marble powder) bound with ~18–20% HDPE resin. It needs no water, no bleach and roughly a quarter of the energy of conventional papermaking, and it is tear-resistant and waterproof — which is why it is gaining share in packaging, labels and premium stationery as plastic bans and ESG mandates tighten.

The global stone paper market is valued at roughly USD 1.06 billion in 2026, growing at ~4.9% CAGR to USD 1.34 billion by 2031, with India the fastest-growing market in Asia-Pacific (~8.8% CAGR, USD 58.7m → USD 122.9m by 2033). A Kishangarh/Rajsamand plant sits inside one of India's densest marble-and-limestone belts — the very slurry that CPCB and NGT currently classify as a pollution hazard becomes your primary feedstock at a fraction of the price quoted in global cost models.

The key finding of the unit-economics work (full detail in [03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md)) is a little counter-intuitive: cheap local limestone lowers your raw-material bill, but it does not dominate it. HDPE resin (imported/domestic petrochemical, not a Rajasthan advantage) is the larger cost line. The Rajasthan-siting advantage is therefore best understood as feedstock security, near-zero logistics cost, a strong circular-economy/ESG story, and access to Rajasthan's RIPS 2024 and Circular Economy Incentive Scheme 2025 subsidies — rather than a silver bullet on cost alone.

Competitively, the field is thin and mostly B2B/packaging-focused globally (TBM, Karst, AM Packaging, Etched Stone Paper) with only one meaningful vertically-branded India player (Ston2pack / Whitestone Stationeries) running both B2B and B2C — and that player appears to have gone dormant (see [04_Competitive_Landscape.md](04_Competitive_Landscape.md) §5.4). No competitor currently claims a captive, mine-mouth position in the Rajasthan marble belt — that is the open white space this idea can occupy.

## 2. The Concept, Briefly

The Instagram reel referenced could not be opened directly (Instagram blocks automated access), so this analysis is built from the underlying "stone paper" category itself, which the reel appears to showcase. The category is well established globally (Taiwan's TBM pioneered it commercially) and is now getting attention in India as a plastic-alternative and tree-free packaging material.

### 2.1 How it's made

* Crush limestone/marble waste and grind to an ultra-fine powder (1,500–2,500 mesh, 6–10 micron).

* Compound the powder (~80%) with HDPE resin (~18%) and additives (~2%) in a high-intensity mixer at 160–180°C.

* Extrude through calibrated dies / blown-film systems into sheets (0.1–0.4mm), then cool, calender and coat for printability.

* Cut and convert into packaging film/board, labels, or stationery (notebooks, diaries, planners).

Because the process uses no water and no wood pulp, it sidesteps two of paper-making's biggest environmental costs — which is also the core of its marketing story. One honesty check worth keeping in the room: the finished product is roughly one-fifth HDPE (a plastic), so "plastic-free" claims need to be phrased carefully ("tree-free / water-free" is accurate and defensible; "plastic-free" is not, and could invite greenwashing pushback).
