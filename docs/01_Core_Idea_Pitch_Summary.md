## 1. Executive Summary

"Stone paper" (also sold as mineral paper) replaces wood pulp with ~80% ground calcium carbonate bound with ~18–20% HDPE resin. No water, no bleach, roughly a quarter of the energy of conventional papermaking, tear-resistant and waterproof — gaining share in packaging, labels and premium stationery as plastic bans and ESG mandates tighten.

Global stone paper: ≈USD 1.06bn (2026) → USD 1.34bn by 2031 (~4.9% CAGR); India is APAC's fastest-growing market (~8.8% CAGR, USD 58.7m → 122.9m by 2033). A Kishangarh/Rajsamand plant sits inside one of India's densest marble-and-limestone belts — the slurry CPCB and NGT classify as a pollution hazard becomes primary feedstock at a fraction of global cost-model prices.

Counter-intuitive finding from the unit-economics work ([03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md)): cheap local limestone lowers the raw-material bill but doesn't dominate it — HDPE resin (a petrochemical, not a Rajasthan advantage) is the larger cost line. The Rajasthan-siting advantage is really feedstock security, near-zero logistics cost, a circular-economy/ESG story, and access to RIPS 2024 and the Circular Economy Incentive Scheme 2025 — not a silver bullet on cost alone.

Competitively, the field is thin, mostly B2B/packaging (TBM, Karst, AM Packaging, Etched Stone Paper globally), with only one branded India player running both B2B/B2C (Ston2pack/Whitestone Stationeries) — and it appears dormant ([04_Competitive_Landscape.md](04_Competitive_Landscape.md) §5.4). No competitor claims a captive, mine-mouth Rajasthan position — the open white space.

## 2. The Concept, Briefly

Built from the underlying "stone paper" category (the source Instagram reel could not be fetched — Instagram blocks automated access). The category is established globally (Taiwan's TBM pioneered it) and is gaining attention in India as a plastic-alternative, tree-free packaging material.

### 2.1 How it's made

* Crush limestone/marble waste, grind to ultra-fine powder (1,500–2,500 mesh, 6–10 micron).
* Compound powder (~80%) with HDPE resin (~18%) and additives (~2%) in a high-intensity mixer at 160–180°C.
* Extrude through calibrated dies/blown-film systems into sheets (0.1–0.4mm), cool, calender, coat for printability.
* Cut and convert into packaging film/board, labels, or stationery.

No water, no wood pulp — the core of the marketing story. Honesty check: the finished product is roughly one-fifth HDPE (plastic), so "tree-free / water-free" is defensible but "plastic-free" is not, and could invite greenwashing pushback.
