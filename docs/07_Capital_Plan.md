# Capital Requirement — A Self-Funded Path, Capped at ₹1.5 Crore Total

*Part of the Limepaper business plan — see [00_README_Index.md](00_README_Index.md) for the full document set. Team is 3: two 18-year-old founders + one senior family member (father) — see [06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) §8.0.*

## 9.1 The hard rule for this plan

**Total capital deployed through loans and finance across the next 36 months — Stage 1 and Stage 2 combined — is capped at ₹1.5 crore. Not a rupee more.** This isn't a target to aim near, it's a ceiling: ₹1.5 crore is the realistic limit of what you can actually raise against your asset base, and going beyond it means personally guaranteeing debt you can't actually back. Every decision below — equipment tier, whether to build or lease, whether to purify feedstock in-house or outsource it — is filtered through staying under this number, not through what would be "ideal" at unlimited capital.

**No expansion beyond this ₹1.5 crore, and no new borrowing, until you've repaid at least 50% of the principal on whatever you do borrow — from actual operating cash flow, not from raising more debt to cover the old debt.** That 50%-repaid milestone is the trigger for even *discussing* a further raise. Until then, the plan runs entirely inside this ₹1.5 crore. What comes after that milestone (a larger, scaled plant) is deliberately not planned out here — there's no value in mapping a ₹15–60 crore expansion before you've proven you can service a fraction of that, and premature big numbers undercut the credibility of the plan, not build it. Revisit that question only once the 50% gate is actually reached.

## 9.2 Stage 1 — The Real Factory: Minimum, Automated, Self-Funded (Months 0–24)

**Goal:** an operating, automated converting factory that starts producing and selling within weeks. Buy finished stone-paper sheet/rolls in bulk from an existing manufacturer and run it through your own automated equipment into finished, sellable products (notebooks, planners, packaging). This is genuinely a factory — capex, a production line, real output, real sales — it's just one stage downstream of raw-material manufacturing, because a raw-material extrusion line starts at ≈₹1.2 crore bare-machine ([03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md) §4.3, §4.6.2) and doesn't leave room for anything else inside a ₹1.5 crore total ceiling on its own, let alone alongside Stage 2's other costs.

### 9.2.1 Budget, automation-first tier (≈₹10–15 lakh)

| Item | Manual/semi-auto tier (rejected) | **Automated tier (recommended)** | Why the automated tier |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Notebook/register making | Separate manual cutting, stitching, folding machines, ₹5–8 lakh combined, needs 3–5 workers across stations | **Single fully-automatic PLC-controlled notebook making machine, ₹6–12 lakh**, feeds paper → cuts → stitches → folds → trims in one line | One trained operator runs the whole line; no multi-station manual labour force to manage, train, or supervise |
| Printing (covers, branding) | Manual/semi-auto printing, ₹3.5–6 lakh, separate step | Outsource to a local printer initially (near-zero capex) | Keeps Stage 1 capex concentrated on the one machine that matters most |
| Packaging/finishing | Manual pasting/packing table | Same — no affordable automated equivalent at this scale, and doesn't need one | Not every step needs a machine |

Indicative Stage 1 spend: entity/trademark/legal (₹30,000–75,000) + compliance/registration (₹20,000–40,000) + automated converting machine (₹6–12 lakh) + finished stone-paper stock for a working production run (₹1.5–3 lakh) + lab testing for B2B credibility (₹30,000–60,000) + working capital/contingency (remainder). Designed to land at or just above ₹10 lakh, covered by your silver (directly or via a silver-backed loan) plus a top-up from your father if needed — this stays comfortably inside the ₹1.5 crore ceiling on its own.

### 9.2.2 Converting ₹10 lakh in silver into working capital

₹10 lakh in silver is an asset, not cash. A silver-backed loan from a bank/NBFC (collateral against the metal, reclaimable once repaid) is usually the better move over selling outright, since Stage 1 revenue can service the interest once sales start. Confirm current LTV and rates directly with a bank/NBFC; not verified in this research pass.

### 9.2.3 Operating philosophy for Stage 1

* **Reinvest, don't extract** — profit goes back into working capital before anyone takes a draw beyond survival needs.
* **Track cost-per-unit, margin per SKU, and machine utilisation from day one** — this is what makes Stage 2 fundable without a consultant reconstructing your numbers.
* **Target: recover the ₹10–15 lakh setup cost within 12 months**, then use months 12–24 to build profit history and 3–5 anchor B2B relationships ([09_B2B_Client_Acquisition.md](09_B2B_Client_Acquisition.md)).
* **No DPR consultant, no PMEGP/CGTMSE application, no loan conversations during Stage 1** — these cost time/money Stage 1 doesn't need yet. Revisit once you're ready to plan Stage 2 within the ₹1.5 crore ceiling.

## 9.3 Stage 2 — Backward Integration into Extrusion, Inside the ₹1.5 Crore Ceiling

Triggered by Stage 1's track record, not a calendar date. The moment you enter Stage 2, every rupee borrowed counts against the same ₹1.5 crore total from §9.1 — Stage 1's ₹10–15 lakh spend is already inside that number, which leaves roughly **₹1.3–1.4 crore for everything in Stage 2**: the extrusion line itself, the shed, utilities, feedstock purification, and working capital.

### 9.3.1 Why feedstock refining has to be budgeted explicitly, not assumed away

The raw material you'd be sourcing locally isn't clean, ready-to-use powder — it's slurry and waste from marble processors *and*, if you also draw from cement-plant-adjacent sources, limestone/CaCO3 byproduct streams that carry a different and often less consistent contaminant profile (kiln dust, silica, mixed mineral content) than pure marble-processing slurry. Both need real purification before they're usable at stone-paper grade (whiteness, particle size, low iron content) — this was flagged as a risk in the original research ([03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md) §4.2, [05_Risks_Diligence.md](05_Risks_Diligence.md)) but needs to be priced now that Stage 2 has a hard ceiling.

Two ways to handle it, with very different capex implications:

* **(a) Outsource purification — recommended given the ₹1.5cr ceiling.** Buy already-purified, micronized stone-paper-grade CaCO3 powder from a local Kishangarh/Rajsamand micronizing unit as a toll-processing or direct-purchase arrangement, at the higher end of the range already cited (₹3,600–9,500/t, [03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md) §4.2) rather than the cheaper raw-slurry price (₹1,000–2,500/t) — that cheaper price is **not usable feedstock** on its own. This turns purification into a per-tonne operating cost, not a capital purchase, which is what actually fits inside the ceiling.
* **(b) Purify in-house.** Buying a beneficiation/grinding line adds real, separately-quoted capex on top of the extrusion machine — likely not affordable inside ₹1.5 crore alongside everything else in Stage 2. Only consider this once you're well past the 50%-repayment gate in §9.1 and planning genuine expansion.
* **Sourcing discipline either way:** prefer marble-only slurry sources over mixed cement-plant waste streams where possible — the purity/consistency is more predictable, which matters more than a small price difference when your margin depends on hitting stone-paper spec every batch. Lab-test every new source before committing to it as a supplier, not just the first sample.

### 9.3.2 Illustrative Stage 2 uses-of-funds, disciplined to the ceiling

| Line item | Illustrative allocation | Notes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Extrusion + calendering line | ₹70–95 lakh | The one IndiaMART listing found for a new 250 kg/h line prices the **bare machine alone** at ₹1.2 crore — that alone would breach what's left of the ceiling once anything else is added. Treat a smaller-capacity or used/refurbished line as the realistic base case, and get 3 firm turnkey quotes before committing to anything. |
| Shed/building | ₹8–10 lakh | Lease and fit out, don't construct — protects capital |
| Utilities connection (power; minimal water) | ₹4–5 lakh | |
| Installation & commissioning | ₹4–6 lakh | |
| Feedstock purification | ₹0 capex | Outsourced per §9.3.1(a) — shows up as a higher per-tonne raw-material cost, not a capital line |
| Working capital (first 3 months) | ₹12–18 lakh | |
| Contingency | ₹8–10 lakh | |
| **Total** | **≈ ₹1.06–1.44 crore** | Stays under the ₹1.5cr ceiling with Stage 1's ₹10–15 lakh included |

*This is illustrative, not a quotation — the extrusion-line price in particular carries real uncertainty (a single unverified listing). The discipline that matters isn't hitting these exact numbers, it's this: whatever real quotes come back, if the total would breach ₹1.5 crore combined with Stage 1, you downgrade equipment tier or defer — you do not raise the ceiling.*

Financing stack: PMEGP subsidy (reduces principal needed) + CGTMSE-backed bank term loan for the balance, with your father as co-applicant/guarantor ([06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) §8.0) strengthening the application. At this point you engage the DPR consultant and compliance/EHS consultant ([06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) roles #1, #3) for the first time at "bankable" depth.

## 9.4 What comes after the 50%-repayment gate

Deliberately not planned out in detail here (§9.1) — once you've repaid at least 50% of the Stage 2 loan principal from real operating cash flow, that's the trigger to revisit expansion, and the numbers at that point should be based on the actual unit economics and cash generation you've proven by then, not a projection made today.

## 9.6 One honest caveat

No stone-paper-specific bulk wholesale price and no verified used-machinery price were found in this research pass — figures above are inferred from adjacent categories. Get three firm quotes for each before finalising any budget, and treat the ₹1.5 crore ceiling as fixed even if quotes come back higher than expected — adjust the equipment tier, not the ceiling.
