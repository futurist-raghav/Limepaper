# Capital Requirement — A Bootstrapped ₹1.5 Crore Plant, Then a ₹15 Crore Raise to Scale

*Part of the Limepaper business plan — see [00_README_Index.md](00_README_Index.md) for the full document set. Team is 3: two 18-year-old founders + one senior family member (father) — see [06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) §8.0. Revised 23 August 2026: the earlier ₹10–15 lakh converting-only first stage has been removed from the plan; the plant now extrudes its own stone paper from Stage 1.*

## 9.1 The structure, in two lines

* **Stage 1 — ₹1.5 crore, bootstrapped.** A small automated extrusion + converting plant in the Kishangarh/Rajsamand belt, funded without outside equity: family capital, the silver asset, and collateral-backed term debt. **₹1.5 crore is a hard ceiling on this stage, not a target.**
* **Stage 2 — ₹15 crore, raised externally.** A 5 TPD plant at the scale India's published project-report benchmark actually costs (₹17.2 crore turnkey — [03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md) §4.3), funded by an equity round rather than more debt.

The old discipline still applies to *borrowing*: no new debt beyond the ₹1.5 crore of Stage 1 until at least 50% of that principal is repaid from operating cash flow (§9.4). Stage 2 is an equity raise, which is why it sits outside that ceiling rather than breaching it — but the ceiling on debt is unchanged, and dilution is the price of keeping it.

## 9.2 Stage 1 — The Plant, Bootstrapped (Months 0–24)

**Goal:** own extrusion from the start. Buy purified, micronized CaCO₃ powder locally, compound it with HDPE, extrude and calender your own sheet, and convert that sheet into finished, sellable product on the same site. One continuous line from local mineral waste to a saleable notebook or carton — which is the only version of this business that owns its margin, its quality and its story end to end.

### 9.2.2 Where ₹1.5 crore actually comes from — and the gap to be honest about

"Bootstrapped" here means *no external equity*, not *no debt*. The realistic stack:

* **₹10 lakh in silver** — an asset, not cash. A silver-backed loan from a bank/NBFC (collateral against the metal, reclaimable once repaid) is usually the better move over selling outright. Confirm current LTV and rates directly with a bank/NBFC; not verified in this research pass.
* **Family contribution (father)** — quantum not yet fixed; this needs a real number before the plan is bankable, because it sets the promoter's margin money.
* **CGTMSE-backed bank term loan** for the balance, with your father as co-applicant/guarantor ([06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) §8.0) strengthening the application. The CGTMSE guarantee ceiling was raised in recent years well above ₹1 crore — confirm the current limit and the bank's own appetite directly, don't plan against a remembered figure.
* **Rajasthan RIPS 2024 + Circular Economy Incentive Scheme 2025** — margin-money assistance, capital subsidy and electricity duty exemption ([03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md) §4.5). These *reduce the principal you need*, they do not arrive as upfront cash.
* **PMEGP is probably not the instrument here.** PMEGP caps project cost for manufacturing units far below ₹1.5 crore (₹50 lakh at last revision — confirm with the District Industries Centre). It was a sensible fit for a ₹10–15 lakh setup; it is not a fit for a ₹1.5 crore plant. Plan the stack around CGTMSE + state incentives and treat PMEGP as out of scope unless the project is deliberately scoped down.

**The honest gap:** ₹10 lakh of silver plus an unquantified family contribution is not ₹1.5 crore. Most of this stage is collateral-backed debt taken on before the business has sold anything. That is a materially different risk profile from the earlier plan, and it is the single most important thing to size properly before signing — see [14_Founder_Reality_Check.md](14_Founder_Reality_Check.md) §6.

### 9.2.3 Why feedstock refining has to be budgeted explicitly, not assumed away

The raw material you'd be sourcing locally isn't clean, ready-to-use powder — it's slurry and waste from marble processors *and*, if you also draw from cement-plant-adjacent sources, limestone/CaCO3 byproduct streams that carry a different and often less consistent contaminant profile (kiln dust, silica, mixed mineral content) than pure marble-processing slurry. Both need real purification before they're usable at stone-paper grade (whiteness, particle size, low iron content) — this was flagged as a risk in the original research ([03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md) §4.2, [05_Risks_Diligence.md](05_Risks_Diligence.md)) but has to be priced now that it sits inside a hard ceiling.

Two ways to handle it, with very different capex implications:

* **(a) Outsource purification — recommended for Stage 1.** Buy already-purified, micronized stone-paper-grade CaCO3 powder from a local Kishangarh/Rajsamand micronizing unit as a toll-processing or direct-purchase arrangement, at the higher end of the range already cited (₹3,600–9,500/t, [03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md) §4.2) rather than the cheaper raw-slurry price (₹1,000–2,500/t) — that cheaper price is **not usable feedstock** on its own. This turns purification into a per-tonne operating cost, not a capital purchase, which is what actually fits inside the ceiling.
* **(b) Purify in-house.** A beneficiation/grinding line is real, separately-quoted capex on top of the extrusion machine — it does not fit inside ₹1.5 crore alongside everything else. This is a **Stage 2 line item** (§9.3), where it also becomes the largest single source of raw-material margin improvement.
* **Sourcing discipline either way:** prefer marble-only slurry sources over mixed cement-plant waste streams where possible — the purity/consistency is more predictable, which matters more than a small price difference when your margin depends on hitting stone-paper spec every batch. Lab-test every new source before committing to it as a supplier, not just the first sample.

### 9.2.4 Operating philosophy for Stage 1

* **Pre-sell before you commission.** With capex now ahead of revenue, 3–5 signed LOIs or pilot POs are no longer a nice-to-have before equipment is ordered ([09_B2B_Client_Acquisition.md](09_B2B_Client_Acquisition.md), [14_Founder_Reality_Check.md](14_Founder_Reality_Check.md)) — they are what stands between a financed plant and an idle one.
* **Reinvest, don't extract** — profit goes back into working capital and debt service before anyone takes a draw beyond survival needs.
* **Track cost-per-tonne, cost-per-SKU, margin per SKU, yield/scrap rate and line utilisation from day one** — this is exactly the data a Stage 2 investor will underwrite, and reconstructing it later costs more than recording it now.
* **Target: full debt service covered from operations, and the line running at a defensible utilisation, before Stage 2 conversations start.**

## 9.3 Stage 2 — The ₹15 Crore Raise

**The ask: ₹15 crore for 10% equity** (implied post-money ₹150 crore). Terms are a founder decision; the use of funds below is what the money buys.

Stage 1 proves the process and the market at small scale on borrowed money. Stage 2 buys the scale at which this category's published economics actually work: India's own project-report benchmark for a **5 TPD (~1,400 t/yr) turnkey stone-paper plant is ≈₹17.2 crore** — land & building ₹2.08cr, plant & machinery ₹12.6cr, three months' working capital ₹2.29cr, with a cited 23% rate of return and 62% break-even point ([03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md) §4.3). A ₹15 crore raise is that plant, financed with equity instead of debt the business can't carry.

### 9.3.1 Illustrative uses of funds

| Line item | Illustrative allocation | What it buys |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Plant & machinery — 5 TPD extrusion, calendering, coating, converting | ₹9.5–11 cr | The capacity step from a bootstrapped starter line to a plant that can quote FMCG-scale volumes |
| In-house micronizing / beneficiation line | ₹1–1.5 cr | Takes feedstock from bought-in powder (₹3,600–9,500/t) to self-processed slurry (₹1,000–2,500/t) — the largest single raw-material margin lever, and the thing that makes the mine-mouth position real rather than rhetorical |
| Land & building | ₹1.5–2.1 cr | Own the site at this scale rather than lease |
| Working capital (3 months) | ₹1.5–2.3 cr | HDPE is bought on short credit and is the largest cost line ([03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md) §4.6.1) |
| Contingency | ₹0.5 cr | |
| **Total** | **≈ ₹14–17 cr** | Anchored to the ₹17.2cr EIRI turnkey benchmark, not to a round number |

*Same discipline as Stage 1: this is illustrative, built from a published project-report benchmark and a single vendor listing, not from quotations. Three firm turnkey quotes before anything is committed.*

### 9.3.2 What has to be true before this raise is credible

An investor will ask for these, and they are the same things you'd want yourself:

* 12+ months of real production and sales from the Stage 1 line — cost per tonne, yield, utilisation, repeat orders.
* 3–5 anchor B2B accounts with repeat purchase history, not LOIs.
* Stage 1 debt serviced on schedule from operations.
* A resolved GST/HSN classification ([05_Risks_Diligence.md](05_Risks_Diligence.md)) — a 5–12% vs. 18% swing moves the margin enough to change the model.
* An answer to why Ston2pack stalled ([04_Competitive_Landscape.md](04_Competitive_Landscape.md) §5.4).

Without those, the ask is a number without a track record behind it — and the valuation is the first thing that gets tested.

## 9.4 The debt gate stays

No new **borrowing** beyond Stage 1's ₹1.5 crore until at least 50% of that principal is repaid from real operating cash flow — not refinanced with new debt. Stage 2 equity does not lift this; it exists precisely so the business doesn't have to breach it.

## 9.6 One honest caveat

No stone-paper-specific bulk wholesale price and no verified used-machinery price were found in this research pass — figures above are inferred from adjacent categories and a single published project report. Get three firm quotes for each before finalising any budget, and treat the ₹1.5 crore Stage 1 ceiling as fixed even if quotes come back higher — adjust the equipment tier, not the ceiling.
