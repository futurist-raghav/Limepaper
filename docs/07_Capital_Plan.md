## 9.1 The structure, in two lines

* **Stage 1 — ₹1.5 crore, bootstrapped.** A lean, essentials-only extrusion + converting plant in Kishangarh/Rajsamand, no outside equity: real family collateral (₹70L shop, ₹10L silver), a soon-clean credit history, and collateral-backed term debt. **₹1.5 crore is a hard ceiling, not a target.**
* **Stage 2 — ₹15 crore, raised externally.** A 5 TPD plant at India's published benchmark cost (₹17.2cr turnkey — [03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md) §4.3), funded by equity, not debt.

No new *borrowing* beyond Stage 1's ₹1.5cr until ≥50% of that principal is repaid from operating cash flow (§9.4). Stage 2 equity sits outside that ceiling rather than breaching it.

## 9.2 Stage 1 — The Plant, Bootstrapped (Months 0–24)

**Goal:** own extrusion from the start, sized to what's financeable. Refine feedstock in-house, compound with HDPE, extrude and calender own sheet, convert into a narrow set of finished products the team already understands — notebooks, cartons, paper bags — with wallpaper and food-contact packaging held back for non-financial reasons.

### 9.2.2 Where ₹1.5 crore actually comes from — and why this version is more credible

"Bootstrapped" means *no external equity*, not *no debt*. This ₹1.5 crore is more realistic than the ₹3 crore version not because it's smaller, but because it's now backed by a real, clear-title asset instead of an unquantified "family contribution."

* **₹70L inherited commercial shop, clear title, father's name, no existing charge** — the single biggest change to the financing story. At a realistic 60–70% LTV for a first-time MSME promoter, this alone supports a **₹42–49L** secured term loan or Loan Against Property. Confirm the bank's actual valuation/LTV directly.
* **₹10L in silver** — unchanged. A silver-backed loan (collateral against the metal, reclaimable once repaid) usually beats selling outright.
* **Combined hard collateral: ≈₹80L.** Promoter margin norms for a loan this size (20–25% of ₹1.5cr = **₹30–37.5L**) are comfortably covered — the direct contrast with ₹3cr, where the same margin math (₹60–75L) vastly exceeded the ₹10L of silver that was the only quantified asset then. That gap is why ₹3cr wasn't achievable; this one closes it.
* **A genuinely clean credit history.** Two existing ₹5L loans, both being closed from this loan's proceeds (₹70,000 + ₹3.8L outstanding — §9.2.1). Showing "closed, not just current" is a stronger position than carrying them forward.
* **Father's carton-industry experience (2022–present, ~4 yrs)** strengthens the DPR and character-based underwriting — a sector-experienced promoter is an easier conversation than two first-time 18-year-olds alone ([06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) §8.0). Not collateral, but a real credibility asset.
* **CGTMSE-backed bank term loan** for the balance beyond property/silver, father as co-applicant/guarantor. Confirm the current guarantee limit and bank appetite directly — CGTMSE covers the gap beyond hard collateral, it doesn't replace quantifying that collateral first.
* **RIPS 2024 + Circular Economy Incentive Scheme 2025** — margin-money assistance, capital subsidy, electricity duty exemption ([03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md) §4.5). These reduce the principal needed; they don't arrive as upfront cash.
* **PMEGP, Mudra, Stand-Up India stay out of scope** at this size (each caps well under ₹1.5cr for a manufacturing project) — don't spend diligence time chasing them.

#### If the core stack still falls short

1. **Machinery-specific financing (hypothecation)** — many vendors, including for the extrusion line, offer financing secured by the machine itself, covering the largest capex line on its own terms.
2. **Split the loan across two lenders** — a bank plus an NBFC, or two banks — rather than one lender for the full ₹1.5cr, especially if comfort with the ₹70L shop as collateral differs by lender.
3. **PSB Loans in 59 Minutes** (psbloansin59minutes.com) — run in parallel with a direct bank relationship.
4. **Lease/hire-purchase the extrusion line** instead of buying outright — converts the biggest capex line into a structured payment obligation, freeing collateral capacity elsewhere.

**Honest read:** ₹80L of hard collateral against a ₹1.5cr ask is a coverage ratio a bank can actually underwrite for a first-time promoter with CGTMSE backing the gap — a fundamentally more fundable conversation than ₹10L of silver against ₹3cr ever was. It's still a real loan against a real family asset, and should be treated that way in every decision from here — [14_Founder_Reality_Check.md](14_Founder_Reality_Check.md) §6.

### 9.2.3 Why feedstock refining is now real capex, not assumed away

Earlier versions treated purification as **₹0 capex** — buy already-purified, micronized powder from a local Kishangarh/Rajsamand unit, purification as a per-tonne opex line. That's still a legitimate, lower-risk default.

This revision adds a **refining/micronizing unit as real capex** (§9.2.1, ₹10–18L) because the founder wants purification in-house, in service of not depending on an outside supplier for the core input:

* **Smaller-scale than the full beneficiation/grinding line scoped as a Stage 2 item** (§9.3.1, ₹1–1.5cr for the 5 TPD version) — sized to Stage 1 throughput. Get a real quote before assuming this number.
* **Sourcing discipline still matters with in-house refining** — prefer marble-only slurry over mixed cement-plant waste streams, lab-test every new source.

### 9.2.4 Operating philosophy for Stage 1

* **The two ring-fenced reserves are not a slush fund.** ₹10L salary/daily-expense buffer and ₹8L emergency reserve exist so payroll and genuine shocks never compete with capex or working capital.
* **Two machines stay funded from operating cash flow, for different reasons.** The label die-cutter (§17.2.2, ₹8–20L) is deferred purely because it wasn't on the founder's Stage 1 list, not risk — fund it first once revenue allows. The mailer-forming unit and wallpaper line stay deferred because each is gated on an unresolved trial or unscoped market — no budget removes that gate.
* **The paper bag machine is budgeted but conditional** (§9.2.1) — "in the budget" isn't "ordered" until the glue/rope trial clears.
* **Pre-sell before commissioning.** 3–5 signed LOIs or pilot POs before equipment is ordered ([09_B2B_Client_Acquisition.md](09_B2B_Client_Acquisition.md), [14_Founder_Reality_Check.md](14_Founder_Reality_Check.md)).
* **Reinvest, don't extract** — profit goes back into working capital and debt service before any draw beyond survival needs.
* **Track cost-per-tonne, cost-per-SKU, margin per SKU, yield/scrap rate, and line utilisation from day one.**
* **Target:** full debt service covered from operations, and defensible line utilisation, before Stage 2 conversations start.

## 9.3 Stage 2 — The ₹15 Crore Raise

**The ask: ₹15 crore for 10% equity** (implied post-money ₹150 crore). Terms are a founder decision; below is what the money buys. Unaffected by this revision — Stage 2 is sized against India's published 5 TPD turnkey benchmark, not Stage 1's ceiling.

Stage 1 proves the process and market at small scale on borrowed money. Stage 2 buys the scale at which this category's published economics work: India's own benchmark for a **5 TPD (~1,400 t/yr) turnkey plant is ≈₹17.2 crore** — land & building ₹2.08cr, plant & machinery ₹12.6cr, three months' working capital ₹2.29cr, cited 23% RoR, 62% break-even ([03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md) §4.3).

*Same discipline as Stage 1: illustrative, from a published benchmark and a single vendor listing, not quotations. Get three firm turnkey quotes before committing anything.*

### 9.3.2 What has to be true before this raise is credible

* 12+ months of real Stage 1 production and sales — cost per tonne, yield, utilisation, repeat orders.
* 3–5 anchor B2B accounts with repeat purchase history, not LOIs.
* Stage 1 debt serviced on schedule from operations.
* A resolved GST/HSN classification ([05_Risks_Diligence.md](05_Risks_Diligence.md)).
* An answer to why Ston2pack stalled ([04_Competitive_Landscape.md](04_Competitive_Landscape.md) §5.4).

Without those, the ask is a number without a track record behind it.

## 9.4 The debt gate stays

No new **borrowing** beyond Stage 1's ₹1.5 crore until ≥50% of that principal (≈₹75L) is repaid from real operating cash flow — not refinanced with new debt. Stage 2 equity does not lift this.

## 9.6 One honest caveat

No stone-paper-specific bulk wholesale price and no verified used-machinery price were found — figures above are inferred from adjacent categories and a single published project report. Get three firm quotes for each before finalising any budget, and treat the ₹1.5cr Stage 1 ceiling as fixed even if quotes come back higher — adjust the equipment tier, not the ceiling. **The ₹42–49L property-loan estimate and the ₹30–37.5L promoter-margin figure are illustrative bank-practice arithmetic, not a quote or valuation from any specific lender — get the shop formally valued and start a real sanction conversation early, since both numbers determine whether ₹1.5cr is financeable at all.**
