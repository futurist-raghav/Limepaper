**Confidence notes:**

* Several figures above (machinery pricing, financing scheme terms, silver loan-to-value) come from single secondary listings or general scheme descriptions rather than verified quotations — flagged inline in the relevant doc where confidence is lower, and worth confirming directly before committing capital.

* No stone-paper-specific bulk wholesale price and no verified used-machinery price were found in this research pass ([07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.6) — get three firm quotes for each before finalising budgets.

* The source Instagram reel (instagram.com/reel/DboGkplzpSl) that originally prompted this research could not be accessed directly due to platform restrictions on automated fetching; this document set is based on the broader stone-paper category the reel appears to reference.

* [13_Paper_Industry_Economics_Deep_Dive.md](13_Paper_Industry_Economics_Deep_Dive.md) revises a claim from earlier drafts: stone paper's capex was previously described as "~40% lower" than conventional paper (§4.6.2 of [03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md)), based on a generic global cost-model article. Real Indian capex data gathered in Doc 13 §13.3.3 shows this holds only against virgin wood-pulp mills, not against the recycled-fibre capacity that actually dominates India's industry (73–76% of furnish) — against that baseline, stone paper's capex per tonne runs higher, not lower. Treat the older claim as superseded by Doc 13, not as an independent second data point.

* No individual Indian-listed paper company publicly discloses per-tonne raw-material/power/employee cost breakdowns at the granularity this research wanted ([13_Paper_Industry_Economics_Deep_Dive.md](13_Paper_Industry_Economics_Deep_Dive.md) §13.3.4) — company-level per-tonne cost figures in Doc 13 are derived/estimated, not disclosed line items, and are flagged as such inline. ITC's Paperboards & Specialty Paper Division full-year segment financials and Trident's isolated paper-segment financials were also not found publicly in this pass — both would need a direct read of the relevant annual report's segment note.

* The GST/HSN classification of stone paper in India is unresolved in public sources ([13_Paper_Industry_Economics_Deep_Dive.md](13_Paper_Industry_Economics_Deep_Dive.md) §13.6) — it could plausibly fall under Chapter 48 (paper, 5–12%), HSN 3921 (plastic sheet/film, 18%), or Chapter 68 (articles of stone). This is flagged as the single highest-priority open regulatory question from all research done to date — get a formal GST advance ruling before finalising pricing or Stage 2 capital commitment.
