# Legal Agreements Library — Checklist & Skeleton Outlines

*Part of the Limepaper business plan — see [00_README_Index.md](00_README_Index.md). Added 11 Sep 2026.*

**Disclaimer — read before using anything below.** This document is a planning and organizational checklist, not legal advice. None of the skeleton outlines in this doc are usable as signable legal documents — they are section lists for a lawyer to draft from, not drafts themselves. Every item in this library requires review and drafting by a licensed Indian company-law/contracts lawyer before execution ([06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) role #4, legal counsel — not yet engaged as of this writing). Treat every "skeleton" heading below as scaffolding for that lawyer conversation, nothing more.

This doc exists because Limepaper currently has zero formal legal documents in place — no entity, no signed agreements, one informal working draft (Doc 15) — and eighteen distinct document types will eventually be needed at different points on the roadmap. The point of this library is sequencing and honesty about status, not legal completeness: it tells the founders what exists, what doesn't, and what actually blocks the next milestone, so legal spend (and the not-yet-hired lawyer's time) gets prioritized against real gates instead of drafted in an arbitrary order.

## 24.2 (1) Founder Agreement

**What it is and why it matters here.** A Founder Agreement is a contract between the people who started the company, signed *before or independent of* the formal SHA, that covers what happens if the working relationship breaks down before there's a company to fall back on — role definitions, time commitment, IP contributed pre-incorporation, and what happens if a founder walks away. For most startups this overlaps heavily with the SHA once incorporated; for Limepaper it matters more, because the three-person team currently includes one member — the father — whose role is genuinely undefined in law: not yet a shareholder, director, employee, or documented advisor, just informally running factory operations and being the presumptive loan guarantor. A Founder Agreement (or an early amendment folded into the SHA) is the document that actually converts "Dad is helping out" into an enforceable, equity-bearing role before real money (the Stage 1 loan) and real decisions depend on him.

**Limepaper-specific status.** Not started. This is the direct legal follow-through on the open item flagged in [06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) §8.0 ("formally involve him in the entity from day one... get CA/legal advice on structure") and the unresolved vesting/exit questions in [15_Equity_Split_Structure.md](15_Equity_Split_Structure.md) §15.3 (items 1 and 5). Nothing has been signed; the current arrangement is entirely informal.

**Skeleton for lawyer review — not a signable legal document.**
- Parties and their stated roles (Raghav, Navya, Sanjay — title/scope for each, incl. Sanjay's operations/guarantor role)
- Time commitment and exclusivity (full-time vs. advisory, moonlighting restrictions)
- Founder equity allocation, referencing the pools in Doc 15 §15.0 pending lawyer conversion
- Vesting schedule and cliff (Doc 15 §15.3 item 1 — including whether Sanjay's stake vests)
- IP assignment carve-in (pre-incorporation work product — cross-ref §24.9 below)
- Decision-making / deadlock mechanism pending full SHA (Doc 15 §15.3 item 3)
- Leaver provisions — good leaver / bad leaver, buyback triggers and pricing (Doc 15 §15.3 item 5)
- Confidentiality between founders and toward outside parties
- Dispute resolution (mediation/arbitration clause, governing law — India)
- Amendment and termination (supersession once SHA and AoA are executed)

## 24.3 (2) Certificate of Incorporation

**What it is and why it matters here.** The Certificate of Incorporation is the document the Registrar of Companies issues confirming the entity legally exists — it is the single fact that everything else in this library (cap table, SHA, contracts signed "by the company" rather than by individuals) depends on. Until this exists, Limepaper is not a legal person: any contract signed today is signed by Raghav, Navya, or Sanjay personally, not by "Limepaper." For a manufacturing business about to take on a ₹1.5 crore secured loan, sign machinery purchase orders, and enter feedstock supply agreements, operating without a corporate entity means every liability sits with the individuals, not a limited-liability company — a materially different risk profile than a pre-revenue SaaS company delaying incorporation.

**Limepaper-specific status.** Not started. A Chartered Accountant engagement for entity structuring is planned as an immediate priority (Doc 6 §8.1 role #2) but has not yet happened. No entity type (private limited company vs. LLP vs. partnership) has been finalized — that decision sits with the CA/lawyer conversation, not this doc.

**Skeleton for lawyer review — not a signable legal document.**
- Entity type decision (private limited company assumed as default for a company planning an equity raise; confirm against Doc 15's dual-pool structure, which is easiest to implement as a private limited company)
- Proposed company name + availability check (RUN/SPICe+ name reservation)
- Registered office address (likely the Kishangarh/Rajsamand factory site or a temporary address)
- Authorized and paid-up share capital at incorporation
- Subscriber/first-director details (Raghav, Navya, and Sanjay's status pending §24.2 resolution)
- DIN/DSC procurement for directors
- SPICe+ filing bundle (MOA, AOA, AGILE-PRO for GST/EPFO/ESIC/bank account, PAN/TAN)
- Post-incorporation compliance calendar starting point (cross-ref §24.19)

## 24.4 (3) Memorandum & Articles of Association (MOA & AOA)

**What it is and why it matters here. **The MOA defines what the company is legally permitted to do (its objects clause) and the AOA is the internal rulebook — how directors are appointed, how board meetings run, how shares transfer, what needs shareholder approval. These are filed with the Registrar and are the *statutory* layer of governance, distinct from the SHA (§24.5), which is a private contract between shareholders. This distinction matters directly for Limepaper's equity structure: Doc 15 §15.2 is explicit that the AoA is where an Option B implementation (equal board seats regardless of shareholding, Reserved Matters requiring supermajority) actually has to be encoded to bind third parties — an SHA alone binds only the signatories, not the company or future shareholders, unless mirrored into the AoA.

**Limepaper-specific status.** Not started. Directly downstream of the entity-structuring decision and the still-open questions in Doc 15 §15.3 (vesting, dilution treatment of the Approval pool, Reserved Matters list, deadlock, exit terms) — none of which can be finalized in the AoA until the founders answer them.

**Skeleton for lawyer review — not a signable legal document.**
- Objects clause (manufacturing/processing of stone paper and derivative products, feedstock refining, converting, wholesale and retail sale — scoped broadly enough to cover both revenue channels in Doc 3 §4.7)
- Share capital structure and classes (ordinary-only per Doc 15 §15.2 Option B, unless a later DVR route is pursued once Option A's profit-track-record condition is met)
- Board composition and appointment/removal mechanics — equal board seats per Doc 15 §15.2 Option B, if that's the route chosen
- Quorum requirements for board and general meetings
- Reserved Matters list requiring unanimous/supermajority consent (Doc 15 §15.3 item 4 — needs to actually be enumerated, not left general)
- Share transfer restrictions (right of first refusal, tag-along/drag-along — none currently defined)
- Dividend policy mechanics
- Winding-up provisions

## 24.5 (4) Shareholders' Agreement (SHA)

**What it is and why it matters here.** The SHA is the private contract between the shareholders that governs control, exit, and decision rights beyond what statute requires — for Limepaper, this is the document that is supposed to make the 33.4/33.3/33.3 Approval-pool intent in Doc 15 actually binding rather than aspirational. Doc 15 §15.2 says this plainly: "until this is written into binding documents, Father's 80% [economic-pool-linked] ordinary equity *is* control." No SHA means no Reserved Matters list, no enforced equal board seats, no deadlock mechanism — the informal 33/33/33 understanding has zero legal weight today.

**Limepaper-specific status.** Partially drafted (informal) — see [15_Equity_Split_Structure.md](15_Equity_Split_Structure.md), which is explicitly labeled "a founders' working draft, not a legal instrument." The substantive content (dual-pool logic, Option A vs. B analysis, five open questions in §15.3) exists; none of it has been converted into a binding document by a lawyer.

**Skeleton for lawyer review — not a signable legal document.**
- Parties (Raghav, Navya, Sanjay — pending §24.2 resolution of Sanjay's formal status) and the company
- Cap table reference (cross-ref §24.6) and the Economic/Approval dual-pool intent (Doc 15 §15.0)
- Confirmation of implementation route — Option A (statutory DVR, likely blocked pre-revenue) vs. Option B (single ordinary class + SHA, recommended) per Doc 15 §15.2
- Board composition — equal seats regardless of shareholding, per Option B
- Reserved Matters list (capital raises, debt/guarantees, hiring/firing founders, business-model changes, related-party contracts, IP transfers, SHA amendments) — currently just a category list in Doc 15 §15.3 item 4, needs full enumeration
- Vesting schedule (Doc 15 §15.3 item 1)
- Dilution mechanics for the Stage 2 raise — does the Approval pool dilute or stay fixed (Doc 15 §15.3 item 2, Doc 7 §9.3)
- Deadlock resolution beyond simple 1-1-1 votes (Doc 15 §15.3 item 3)
- Leaver/exit/buyout terms — valuation method, triggering events (Doc 15 §15.3 item 5)
- Non-compete / non-solicit between founders
- Information rights, veto rights, anti-dilution (if/when investors join per §24.18)
- Governing law, dispute resolution

## 24.6 (5) Cap Table

**What it is and why it matters here.** The cap table is the authoritative record of who owns what — not a legal document in itself, but the single source of truth that the AoA, SHA, ESOP pool, and any future investment agreement all have to reconcile against. Getting it wrong or leaving it informal is how startups end up with disputes over who actually owns what percentage after a raise. For Limepaper, the cap table has to track two things simultaneously per Doc 15's dual-pool structure — Economic ownership and Approval/voting rights — which is not the shape of a standard single-class cap table template and needs explicit lawyer/CA sign-off on how it's structured and filed.

**Limepaper-specific status.** Partially drafted (informal) — the 70/15/15 Economic and 33.4/33.3/33.3 Approval split is specified in [15_Equity_Split_Structure.md](15_Equity_Split_Structure.md) §15.0, but as a working draft, not a filed or lawyer-reviewed cap table. No actual share certificates or register exist because no entity exists yet.

**Skeleton for lawyer review — not a signable legal document.**
- Current holders and Economic-pool percentages (Father 70%, Raghav 15%, Navya 15% — Doc 15 §15.0)
- Approval-pool percentages if implemented as a separate tracked mechanism (33.4/33.3/33.3) or folded entirely into SHA-level board/veto rights instead of a share-level construct
- Authorized vs. issued vs. outstanding shares
- Option pool carve-out, if any, ahead of Stage 2 (Doc 15 §15.3 item 2 flags this needs deciding before the raise)
- Fully diluted view modeling the Stage 2 ₹15cr-for-10% raise (Doc 7 §9.3)
- Vesting status per holder, once §24.2/§24.5 vesting terms are set
- Version history / amendment log (cap tables change — keep dated versions, not overwrites)

## 24.7 (6) ESOP Policy

**What it is and why it matters here.** An ESOP (employee stock option plan) lets a company grant equity to employees as compensation, typically vesting over time — standard for attracting talent a cash-constrained startup can't pay full market rate for. It matters less urgently for Limepaper than for a typical tech startup: Stage 1 hiring per [06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) §8.1 and §8.3 is 2–4 trained machine operators (ITI/diploma-level) plus role-specific consultants, not the kind of senior technical hires where equity comp is the norm or expectation. It becomes relevant later, if/when Limepaper needs to attract a process engineer, plant manager, or senior commercial hire it can't otherwise afford — or if an investor (e.g., Antler) expects an option pool to exist as a condition of the raise.

**Limepaper-specific status.** Not started, and not urgent. No hires to date require equity compensation; the current hiring plan (Doc 6 §8.1) is operator- and consultant-level, paid in cash/retainer, not equity.

**Skeleton for lawyer review — not a signable legal document.**
- Pool size (typically 5–15% of fully diluted equity — decide against Doc 15's dilution treatment, §15.3 item 2)
- Eligibility criteria (which future roles qualify — likely senior technical/commercial hires only, not floor operators)
- Vesting schedule (standard 4-year/1-year cliff, matching founder vesting terms once set)
- Exercise price and exercise window post-departure
- Trust structure vs. direct grant (SEBI/company law mechanics — lawyer/CA call)
- Board approval and shareholder resolution requirements for pool creation and individual grants

## 24.8 (7) Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA)

**What it is and why it matters here.** An NDA restricts a counterparty from disclosing or using confidential information shared with them. For Limepaper this is not boilerplate — the feedstock supplier relationships and the in-house refining/extrusion process are explicitly identified as the closest thing this business has to a moat ([22_Business_Frameworks_Compendium.md](22_Business_Frameworks_Compendium.md) §22.2: "vertical integration... genuinely more integrated than the pattern the competitive landscape describes... capital-intensive to replicate, but replicable — it's operational discipline and capital, not a secret"). An NDA doesn't create IP protection where none exists (there's no patent here — §22.2 is explicit stone paper extrusion is commodity technology), but it does matter for anything that *is* Limepaper-specific: supplier pricing terms, customer lists, sample formulations, process tuning learned through trial and error, and financial data shared with prospective investors or lenders.

**Limepaper-specific status.** Not started. Needed before any substantive conversation with a machinery vendor, a feedstock supplier beyond an initial cold approach, a prospective investor (including Antler diligence, Doc 20), or a prospective B2B customer receiving sample specs/pricing.

**Skeleton for lawyer review — not a signable legal document.**
- Parties and definition of "Confidential Information" (scoped to cover process/formulation detail, supplier terms, pricing, customer data, financials)
- Mutual vs. one-way (mutual likely needed for investor/vendor conversations; one-way may suffice for early customer sampling)
- Permitted use and disclosure exceptions (publicly available info, independently developed, legally compelled disclosure)
- Term (typically 2–5 years post-disclosure)
- Return/destruction of information on request or relationship end
- Remedies (injunctive relief clause, given information leakage can't be "undone" by damages alone)
- Governing law, dispute resolution

## 24.9 (8) Intellectual Property (IP) Assignment Agreement

**What it is and why it matters here.** An IP Assignment Agreement ensures that anything created by a founder, employee, or contractor for the business — formulations, process improvements, branding, product designs — legally belongs to the company, not to the individual who made it. Without it, IP created before incorporation (formulation trials, brand name/logo work, early product SKU designs per [18_Product_SKU_Catalog.md](18_Product_SKU_Catalog.md)) legally sits with whichever founder created it personally, not with "Limepaper." As noted in §24.8, the underlying stone-paper process is commodity technology with no patent moat — but branding, specific SKU designs, and any process refinements Limepaper does develop internally are still assets worth formally owning at the company level rather than leaving attributed to an individual.

**Limepaper-specific status.** Not started. Relevant immediately at incorporation for pre-incorporation work product (cross-ref §24.2), and again at every future hire or contractor engagement (§24.10, §24.11) where the person might create anything IP-relevant (packaging design, marketing content, process documentation, a website).

**Skeleton for lawyer review — not a signable legal document.**
- Definition of "Work Product" / "Invention" (formulations, process documentation, designs, brand assets, software/website, written content)
- Present and future assignment clause (assigns IP as created, not just IP existing at signing)
- Moral rights waiver where applicable
- Carve-out for pre-existing IP the individual brings in (disclosed and excluded, not silently swept in)
- Cooperation clause (signing further documents if needed to perfect the assignment, e.g., trademark filings)
- Trigger for trademark registration of "Limepaper" brand name/logo, once decided

## 24.10 (9) Employment Agreement

**What it is and why it matters here.** A standard employment contract covering role, compensation, confidentiality, IP assignment, and termination terms. For Limepaper this is a manufacturing-floor document first, not a knowledge-worker one: the first hires are 2–4 trained machine operators for extrusion and converting lines ([06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) §8.1 role #9, §8.3), where the employment terms that matter most are things like shift structure, equipment-handling responsibility, safety/EHS compliance obligations, and standard Indian labour-law entitlements (PF/ESI, working hours under the Factories Act) — not the equity/IP-heavy template startup employment agreements are usually built around.

**Limepaper-specific status.** Not started. Needed before Stage 1 commissioning hiring begins (Doc 6 §8.1 role #9 — "hired ahead of install").

**Skeleton for lawyer review — not a signable legal document.**
- Role, reporting line, probation period
- Compensation, statutory benefits (PF, ESI, gratuity eligibility thresholds)
- Working hours, shift structure, overtime (Factories Act compliance — relevant given this is a plant with extrusion/converting lines running shifts)
- Confidentiality and IP assignment clauses (cross-ref §24.8, §24.9)
- Safety/EHS compliance obligations specific to a marble-slurry-feedstock, HDPE-extrusion plant (cross-ref Doc 6 §8.1 role #3)
- Termination terms, notice period
- Non-compete/non-solicit (scoped narrowly — enforceability of broad non-competes is limited under Indian law)
- Grievance/dispute mechanism

## 24.11 (10) Contractor/Freelancer Agreement

**What it is and why it matters here.** Covers engagements structured as independent contractor relationships rather than employment — relevant for Limepaper's several planned consultant engagements (DPR consultant, process engineer, EHS consultant, mineral QC specialist — [06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) §8.1 roles #1, #3, #5, #6) that are explicitly one-off or project-based rather than full-time hires. Getting the classification right matters: misclassifying what's functionally an employee as a contractor creates real labour-law exposure, and several of these roles ("Paid consultant through commissioning; full-time once volume justifies it" for role #5) may shift from contractor to employee status over time.

**Limepaper-specific status.** Not started. Needed immediately — several of these consultant engagements (DPR consultant, EHS consultant) are flagged in Doc 6 §8.1 as needed "immediately," ahead of any employee hiring.

**Skeleton for lawyer review — not a signable legal document.**
- Scope of work, deliverables, timeline (specific to the engagement — e.g., DPR drafting, EHS consent filing, process engineering review)
- Fee structure and payment schedule (one-off fee per Doc 6 §8.1, e.g. ₹50,000–2L for the DPR consultant)
- Independent contractor status confirmation (no employment relationship, no benefits obligation) — drafted to reflect actual working reality, not just labeled as such
- IP assignment for deliverables (cross-ref §24.9 — especially relevant for the DPR itself and any process documentation)
- Confidentiality (cross-ref §24.8 — consultants will see process/financial detail)
- Termination for convenience / for cause
- Indemnity and liability caps, particularly relevant for the EHS/compliance consultant given regulatory exposure

## 24.12 (11) Vendor Agreement

**What it is and why it matters here.** This is one of the highest-priority document types on this entire list for Limepaper specifically, and should not be treated with the generic urgency a SaaS-startup checklist would assign it. Two categories of vendor relationship carry real capital and operating risk: (1) the machinery/EPC vendor for the ₹55–70L extrusion line and converting equipment ([06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) §8.1 role #7 — "Distinguishing a credible line vendor from an IndiaMART listing photo... before *any* capex commitment"), and (2) feedstock suppliers — the marble/limestone slurry processors and HDPE distributors that [22_Business_Frameworks_Compendium.md](22_Business_Frameworks_Compendium.md) §22.2 identifies as Limepaper's actual moat. [12_Vendor_Hiring_Due_Diligence.md](12_Vendor_Hiring_Due_Diligence.md) already lays out the due-diligence checklist for evaluating these vendors; this document is the contractual layer that has to follow that diligence — specification, warranty, AMC/spares/training terms for machinery, and offtake volume/quality/pricing terms for feedstock.

**Limepaper-specific status.** Not started. Needed before any machinery purchase commitment (the single largest capital outlay in Stage 1, Doc 7 §9.2.1) and before any feedstock supply commitment.

**Skeleton for lawyer review — not a signable legal document.**
- Machinery vendor track: equipment specification, performance guarantees (throughput, output quality specs), delivery/installation timeline, payment milestones tied to commissioning, warranty period, AMC/spares/training terms (Doc 6 §8.1 role #7), penalty clauses for delay
- Feedstock vendor track: quality specs (CaCO₃ purity, whiteness index, particle size — Doc 6 §8.1 role #6), volume commitments, pricing and revision mechanism (especially relevant given HDPE price volatility, Doc 22 §22.1 cost structure), delivery terms, exclusivity or non-exclusivity
- Force majeure and supply-disruption provisions
- Inspection/rejection rights and quality dispute resolution
- Payment terms and security (advance payment protection for machinery, given capital is collateral-backed debt)
- Termination and transition-out provisions
- Governing law, dispute resolution/arbitration venue

## 24.13 (12) Customer Agreement

**What it is and why it matters here.** Governs the terms of sale with B2B customers — the converters and brands buying raw substrate (Channel 1) and the corporate-gifting/branded-goods buyers (Channel 2), per [03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md) §4.7's two-channel model. This matters because B2B industrial sales in this category run on technical qualification and spec compliance, not a simple purchase order — [22_Business_Frameworks_Compendium.md](22_Business_Frameworks_Compendium.md) §22.3 describes the "switching cost" story as built on formal requalification (spec, tolerance, tear/waterproof testing), which means the customer agreement needs to capture spec commitments and quality remedies precisely, not generically.

**Limepaper-specific status.** Not started. Needed before the first LOI converts into an actual signed sale — [09_B2B_Client_Acquisition.md](09_B2B_Client_Acquisition.md) is the sales playbook; this is the contract that has to exist once that playbook produces a real order. As of the most recent revision (Doc 22 §22.1), Limepaper has zero signed LOIs.

**Skeleton for lawyer review — not a signable legal document.**
- Product specification and tolerance commitments (thickness, tear resistance, waterproofing — matched to whatever spec sheet is used for buyer qualification)
- Pricing, payment terms, credit terms if any
- Volume commitments / minimum order quantities, if applicable
- Delivery, packaging, and logistics terms
- Quality inspection and rejection/return rights
- Liability and indemnity caps (limiting exposure if a batch fails downstream in a customer's process)
- IP/confidentiality carve-out if the customer shares proprietary specs (cross-ref §24.8)
- Termination and dispute resolution

## 24.15 (14) Privacy Policy

**What it is and why it matters here.** A Privacy Policy discloses what personal data a business collects and how it's used — a legal requirement for any website or app that collects user data, and a major compliance surface for consumer-facing businesses. For Limepaper, honestly, this matters far less than for a consumer app: the business is B2B manufacturing selling to corporate buyers and converters, with personal data exposure limited to basic contact details of business counterparties (vendor contacts, customer procurement contacts, job applicants) rather than any consumer-scale data collection. It should not be treated with the same urgency the other 17 items in this list carry.

**Limepaper-specific status.** Not started, and low-priority relative to the rest of this list. Becomes relevant only once Limepaper operates a website that collects visitor/lead data, or a D2C channel that collects consumer order data — neither exists yet.

**Skeleton for lawyer review — not a signable legal document.**
- Categories of personal data collected (contact forms, job applications, D2C order data if that channel is built)
- Purpose of collection and use
- Data sharing with third parties (payment processors, logistics providers, if applicable)
- Data retention and deletion practices
- User rights (access, correction, deletion) under applicable Indian law
- Cookie/tracking disclosure if a marketing website with analytics is built
- Grievance officer contact (required under Indian IT Rules for any data-collecting website)

## 24.16 (15) Data Protection & Compliance Documents

**What it is and why it matters here.** Covers formal compliance with India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDPA) and related IT Rules — internal data-handling policies, breach-notification procedures, data processing agreements with vendors who touch personal data. Like the Privacy Policy above, this matters far less for Limepaper than for a data-driven consumer or SaaS business: there is no large-scale personal data processing anywhere in this business model. The actual "data" that matters to Limepaper's operations is production/quality data, unit economics (Doc 20's metrics framework), and supplier/customer business information — not personal data subject to DPDPA in any meaningful volume. This item exists on the checklist for completeness, not because it's a near-term priority.

**Limepaper-specific status.** Not started, and correctly the lowest-priority item on this entire list. No significant personal-data-processing activity exists in the current business model. Revisit only if a D2C consumer channel with real transaction volume is built, or if HR data volume grows meaningfully with headcount.

**Skeleton for lawyer review — not a signable legal document.**
- Internal data-handling policy (what employee/HR data is collected, storage, access controls)
- DPDPA applicability assessment (likely minimal exposure given B2B model — confirm with lawyer once/if D2C channel exists)
- Data processing agreements with any third-party vendor handling data on Limepaper's behalf (payroll processor, if outsourced)
- Breach notification procedure
- Data retention schedule

## 24.17 (16) Board Resolutions

**What it is and why it matters here.** Board resolutions are the formal record of decisions the board of directors is legally required to approve — opening a bank account, taking on debt, appointing officers, approving major contracts. For Limepaper, this becomes operationally important almost immediately after incorporation: the ₹1.5 crore Stage 1 term loan ([07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.2.2) will require board approval to authorize borrowing and pledge collateral, machinery purchase orders above a threshold will typically need board sign-off, and — per Doc 15 §15.2's Option B — this is also where the "equal board seats regardless of shareholding" governance intent actually gets exercised in practice, not just written into the AoA.

**Limepaper-specific status.** Not started. The first board resolution will be the incorporation-adjacent set (bank account opening, appointment of first officers); the first *substantive* one will almost certainly be authorizing the Stage 1 loan and pledging the ₹70L shop/silver as collateral.

**Skeleton for lawyer review — not a signable legal document.**
- Standard resolution template structure (recitals, resolved clauses, certification, board meeting minutes reference)
- Bank account opening and signatory authorization (first resolution needed)
- Loan/borrowing authorization and collateral pledge (Stage 1 term loan, Doc 7 §9.2.2)
- Appointment of key managerial personnel
- Approval of related-party transactions (relevant given father's dual role as operator/possible director/guarantor)
- Approval of major vendor/capex contracts above a defined threshold
- Annual resolutions (financial statements approval, AGM-related, once applicable)

## 24.18 (17) Share Subscription & Investment Agreements

**What it is and why it matters here.** These are the documents that formalize an equity investment — a Share Subscription Agreement (SSA) governing the actual share issuance and an accompanying investment/shareholders' agreement layering investor rights (board seats, information rights, protective provisions, liquidation preference) on top. For Limepaper this is currently hypothetical but live: the Antler India pre-seed track (~₹2–4cr for 9–11% equity, referenced in [20_Data_Metrics_Framework.md](20_Data_Metrics_Framework.md)) is "a possibility, not a commitment" — an undecided question elsewhere in this planning process — and the Stage 2 raise (₹15cr for 10%, [07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.3) is a firmer future milestone. Either would require this document category, and either would also force resolution of the still-open dilution question in Doc 15 §15.3 item 2 (does the Approval pool dilute with an outside raise, or stay fixed among the three founders) before terms could be signed.

**Limepaper-specific status.** Not started. No investor conversation has produced a term sheet as of this writing. If the Antler track (or any other pre-seed conversation) is pursued, this becomes the fastest-moving item on the whole list — investment documentation typically has to be turned around in weeks once a term sheet is agreed, faster than most of the incorporation-cluster items above.

**Skeleton for lawyer review — not a signable legal document.**
- Term sheet terms translated into binding form (valuation, investment amount, equity percentage, instrument type — equity vs. CCPS/CCD)
- Share Subscription Agreement (subscription price, closing conditions, representations and warranties)
- Investor rights (board seat or observer rights, information rights, pro-rata/anti-dilution rights)
- Interaction with the existing dual-pool structure (Doc 15) — does investor equity come out of the Economic pool only, and does the Approval pool stay fixed at 33.4/33.3/33.3 among founders regardless (Doc 15 §15.3 item 2 — needs an explicit answer before signing, "it won't happen by default")
- Protective provisions / investor veto rights, reconciled against the founders' existing Reserved Matters list (§24.5)
- Use-of-funds covenant
- Conditions precedent and closing mechanics

## 24.19 (18) Statutory & Regulatory Compliance Records

**What it is and why it matters here.** This is the ongoing recordkeeping layer — GST filings, Udyam/MSME registration, PCB Consent to Establish and Operate, Factories Act registration, ROC annual filings, and the paper trail proving compliance across all of these. Unlike the other 17 items, this isn't a single document but a continuously accumulating file. It matters intensely for Limepaper's specific risk profile: [06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) §8.1 role #3 flags that "marble slurry feedstock is CPCB/NGT-watched; no PCB Consent to Establish without this. Extrusion from Stage 1 triggers full consent path (Establish + Operate) on day one" — this is not generic paperwork, it's a genuine gating requirement before the plant can legally operate.

**Limepaper-specific status.** Not started. GST/Udyam registration is planned as an immediate CA-engagement priority (Doc 6 §8.1 role #2) but not yet done. PCB Consent to Establish is flagged as immediately necessary once the EHS consultant is engaged (Doc 6 §8.1 role #3), ahead of any equipment installation.

**Skeleton for lawyer review — not a signable legal document.**
- GST registration and ongoing return filing schedule
- Udyam (MSME) registration — relevant to subsidy/scheme eligibility (RIPS/Circular Economy Incentive Scheme, cross-ref Doc 22 §22.1)
- PCB Consent to Establish, then Consent to Operate (full path given extrusion from Stage 1 — Doc 6 §8.1 role #3)
- Factories Act registration and labour law compliances (linked to headcount, cross-ref §24.10)
- ROC annual filings (AOC-4, MGT-7, once incorporated)
- Trademark registration for the Limepaper brand name/logo, if pursued (cross-ref §24.9)
- Fire/electrical/local trade licence approvals for the factory site
- A maintained compliance calendar — the actual deliverable of this item, updated as each filing type comes due
