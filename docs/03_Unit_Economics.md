# Unit Economics — Siting Near Kishangarh/Rajsamand, and vs. Wood-Pulp Paper

*Part of the Limepaper business plan — see [00_README_Index.md](00_README_Index.md) for the full document set.*

## 4.1 Why the location argument holds — and where it doesn't

Kishangarh ("Marble City of India") and Rajsamand sit at the centre of Rajasthan's marble and limestone processing belt, which also supplies large cement players (Shree Cement, JK, Ambuja/UltraTech units nearby). Two facts matter for a stone-paper plant specifically:

* Marble slurry is currently a disposal liability, not just a cheap input. Kishangarh's dedicated slurry dumping yard (82 acres) receives a truck roughly every 10 minutes at peak, and CPCB/NGT have flagged groundwater and soil contamination — processors are actively looking for offtake, which means feedstock supply agreements can plausibly be negotiated cheaply or even on a "we-collect-it-for-you" basis.

* Local market pricing confirms the gap: raw marble slurry/dust trades around ₹1,000–2,500 per tonne in the Kishangarh/Alwar/Makrana belt, and locally milled micronized calcium carbonate powder (the finer grade closer to what stone paper needs) trades around ₹3,600–9,500 per tonne depending on fineness/purity — versus a global benchmark of USD 50–200 (≈₹4,750–19,000) per tonne for industrial-waste-grade calcium carbonate. Rajasthan sourcing sits at or below the low end of the global range.

* The counter-point: calcium carbonate is only ~80% of the finished product's mass, but because it is so cheap even at "global" prices, HDPE resin (~18% of mass, priced USD 987–1,277 ≈ ₹94,000–121,000 per tonne, sourced from national petrochemical majors, not a local input) is the larger line item in raw-material cost. Being near the mines mainly compresses an already-small cost line — its bigger value is supply security, near-zero freight on your bulkiest input, and the ESG/circular-economy narrative described below.

* **A caveat that changes the real cost, not just the theory:** "cheap local limestone" assumes usable, on-spec limestone. In practice, marble-processing slurry and any limestone/CaCO3 byproduct sourced from cement-plant-adjacent streams both carry real impurities — mixed marble/granite dust, kiln dust, silica, iron content — and cement-plant-adjacent sources in particular can be less consistent than pure marble-processing slurry. None of it is usable at stone-paper grade (whiteness index, particle size, low contaminant content) without a genuine purification/beneficiation step. See §4.2.1 for how this is actually priced in, rather than assumed away.

## 4.2 Illustrative raw-material cost per tonne of finished stone paper

Based on the commonly cited formulation of 0.80t calcium carbonate + 0.18t HDPE + 0.02t additive per tonne of output, applied to current Rajasthan market prices (August 2026, USD–INR ≈ 95):

| Input | Qty per tonne output | Unit price (Rajasthan) | Cost contribution |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Calcium carbonate powder | 0.80t | ₹1,750–4,750/t (raw slurry to milled powder) | ₹1,400–3,800 |
| HDPE resin | 0.18t | ₹94,000–121,000/t | ₹16,900–21,800 |
| Additives/coating | 0.02t | ₹50,000–80,000/t (indicative) | ₹1,000–1,600 |
| **Estimated raw-material cost/tonne** | — | — | **≈ ₹19,000–27,000** |

*This is a bottom-up illustrative estimate built from public formulation ratios and current marketplace listings, not a supplier quotation. HDPE and additive costs move with crude/petrochemical cycles; get firm quotes from Reliance/GAIL-linked distributors and 2–3 local micronizing units before underwriting a plan. Get local calcium carbonate suppliers to quote specifically at the 1,500–2,500 mesh / high-whiteness grade stone paper needs — raw marble slurry is not directly usable without purification and fine grinding, which is itself a meaningful capex/opex line (see §4.2.1).*

### 4.2.1 Refining/purification — the cost the ₹1,400–3,800 line above hides

The calcium carbonate row in the table above spans a wide price range (₹1,750–4,750/t) for a reason: the low end (≈₹1,000–2,500/t) is **unprocessed raw slurry — not usable as stone-paper feedstock on its own.** The realistic input cost is the higher end of the range and beyond — locally milled, micronized, purity-tested powder at ≈₹3,600–9,500/t — because getting from raw slurry to that spec requires an actual purification/beneficiation step: washing, fine grinding to 1,500–2,500 mesh, whiteness/purity testing, and rejecting or blending out contaminated batches.

Two ways to get purification done, with very different cost profiles — full financial treatment in [07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.3.1:

* **Outsource it** — buy already-purified, micronized powder from a local Kishangarh/Rajsamand micronizing unit, paying the higher ₹3,600–9,500/t price as your real per-tonne raw-material cost. No separate capex; the "refining cost" is simply the gap between raw-slurry and milled-powder pricing above.
* **Do it in-house** — buy raw slurry cheap and add your own beneficiation/grinding line. Adds real, separately-quoted capital equipment on top of your extrusion line — a meaningful capex decision in its own right, not a rounding error.

**Sourcing note:** marble-processing slurry and cement-plant-adjacent limestone/CaCO3 byproduct streams are not interchangeable — the latter can carry a different, less consistent contaminant profile (kiln dust, silica). Lab-test every new source, including repeat batches from an existing supplier, rather than assuming one good sample means the relationship is reliable.

| Resource | Stone paper | Conventional wood-pulp paper | Why it matters here |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |

## 4.5 Rajasthan policy tailwinds

* **Rajasthan Investment Promotion Scheme (RIPS) 2024–2029:** standard incentive package for new manufacturing units — investment subsidy, capital subsidy and turnover-linked incentives, plus interest subsidy and skill-development support for MSMEs. Confirm exact slab percentages for your investment size with the District Industries Centre / RIICO before finalising the plan.

* **Rajasthan Circular Economy Incentive Scheme 2025:** this is the standout fit. Units where at least 51% (by weight) of raw material is recycled/reused waste qualify — a plant built on marble/limestone slurry clears this bar comfortably (your CaCO3 alone is ~80% of input mass). Benefits include full RIPS-2024 access plus "thrust sector" status for the first 5 approved units, margin-money assistance of 25% (micro, up to ₹15 lakh/yr) or 15% (small, up to ₹20 lakh/yr) for the first 20 MSMEs (with an extra ₹5 lakh ceiling for SC/ST/PwD or young entrepreneurs), 50% technology-acquisition assistance (up to ₹5 lakh/yr) for the first 20 startups, and electricity duty exemptions.

* Being an early mover matters here: several of these benefits are explicitly capped at "first 5" or "first 20" approved units — worth engaging Rajasthan's Department of Industries early to understand where the queue currently stands.

## 4.6 Stone Paper vs. Wood-Pulp Paper — Full Unit Economics Comparison

This is the comparison worth underwriting a plan on: not "is stone paper cheap" in isolation, but how its resource-and-capital profile stacks up against the wood-pulp paper it's meant to displace.

### 4.6.1 Per-tonne materials & energy cost

| Cost element | Wood-pulp paper (per tonne output) | Stone paper (per tonne output) | Notes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Primary raw material | ~4t pulpwood @ ₹8,000–15,000/t = **₹32,000–60,000** | 0.80t CaCO₃ @ ₹1,750–4,750/t = **₹1,400–3,800** | Wood cost has itself surged ~88% (₹8,000→₹15,000/t) in recent industry reporting; India also pays a premium (~USD50/t) vs. some international pulpwood buyers (~USD30/t). |
| Binder / process chemicals | Pulping & bleaching chemicals — folded into "chemicals" below, not separately broken out here | 0.18t HDPE @ ₹94,000–121,000/t = **₹16,900–21,800** | HDPE is a national petrochemical commodity, not a Rajasthan-sourced input — this is stone paper's largest and most volatile cost line, not the mineral. |
| Additives / coating | included in chemicals line | 0.02t @ ~₹50,000–80,000/t = **₹1,000–1,600** | |
| **Raw-material subtotal** | **≈ ₹32,000–60,000+ (wood alone; pulping chemicals extra, typically 5–10% of total opex)** | **≈ ₹19,000–27,000** | Even with expensive HDPE, stone paper's material bill runs roughly half of wood-pulp paper's. |
| Process water | ~200 tonnes/tonne, plus the effluent-treatment plant (ETP) capex and opex this volume mandates | ~0 | The single biggest structural advantage for a water-stressed state like Rajasthan — not just a cost line but a siting and community-relations constraint stone paper avoids entirely. |
| Energy | ~1,300–1,700 kWh/t electricity **plus** 11–15 tonnes/t of steam (coal/biomass boiler) — electricity alone ≈ **₹9,750–12,750** at ₹7.5/kWh industrial tariff, before steam-raising cost | ~700 kWh/t, **no boiler/steam stage at all** ≈ **₹5,250** | Energy runs 16–25% of total wood-pulp production cost industry-wide; stone paper's process (grind → compound → extrude) skips pulping, bleaching and drying entirely. |
| **Illustrative all-in cost (materials + energy only)** | **≈ ₹42,000–73,000+** | **≈ ₹24,000–32,000** | Excludes labour, maintenance and depreciation — those apply to both processes roughly proportionally and are not broken out here. |

*On these figures stone paper's variable cost per tonne runs roughly 40–55% below wood-pulp paper's — a genuinely different story from the "cheap limestone" framing in §4.1. The saving comes mostly from water and energy structurally, and from wood being an increasingly expensive, security-exposed input (India's pulpwood cost has been rising and is partly imported). HDPE exposure is the one place stone paper inherits a volatility risk wood-pulp paper doesn't have.*

*For reference, the wider cost-structure split reported for recycled-paper mills in India (raw materials 55–65%, utilities 20–25%, labour 5–8%, chemicals 5–10%, maintenance 3–5%, other 3–5% of total opex) is a useful sanity check on the percentages above, though it's a different cost base (recycled fibre, not virgin pulpwood).*

### 4.6.2 Capex — and a discrepancy worth flagging before you rely on either number

An IndiaMART listing prices a 250 kg/hr stone-paper extrusion line (≈1,800 t/yr at full utilisation — comparable scale to the EIRI 5 TPD / ~1,400 t/yr benchmark in §4.3) at **₹1.2 crore**. That is roughly **14x cheaper** than the ₹17.2 crore EIRI turnkey benchmark for a similar-scale plant. The gap isn't a contradiction — it's scope: the ₹1.2cr figure is very likely the bare extrusion machine from a single unverified vendor listing, excluding land, building, the mineral micronizing/grinding line, calendering/coating/finishing equipment, utilities and commissioning — all of which the EIRI DPR figure includes. **Treat ₹1.2cr as a "core machine only" floor and ₹17.2cr as the realistic turnkey planning number** until you have a firm, itemised, all-in quote (see [06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) role #7).

At the ~5,000 t/yr scale, the global benchmark already cited in §4.3 holds: a stone-paper plant (≈USD 6.37m / ₹60.5cr) costs roughly 40% less to build than an equivalent wood-pulp mill of the same output (≈USD 10.6m / ₹100cr) — capex advantage, payback advantage (2–3 years vs. 4–6), and the operating-cost advantage in §4.6.1, all point the same direction. That said, this scale of plant isn't part of the current plan: the current strategy deliberately caps total borrowed capital at ₹1.5 crore for the next 36 months and only revisits any larger expansion after a specific loan-repayment milestone is hit — see [07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.1. The EIRI (₹17.2cr) and global (₹60.5cr) benchmarks here are kept as market reference points, not as numbers this plan is currently working toward.
