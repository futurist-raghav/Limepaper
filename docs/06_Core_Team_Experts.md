## 8.0 Your existing team of three

Not really "two founders" — three: two 18-year-old co-founders and one senior family member (father), the most experienced person on the team.

* **Formally involve him in the entity from day one** (co-founder, director, or documented advisor with equity — get CA/legal advice on structure). The ₹1.5 crore Stage 1 term loan ([07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.2.2) needs a co-applicant/guarantor with more credit/asset history than two 18-year-olds — a day-one requirement, not year-two.
* **He brings ~4 years of carton-industry experience (2022–present),** a genuine credibility asset for the lender conversation and the DPR — a promoter with sector-relevant operating experience is a materially easier underwrite. It's also why his hands-on involvement matters most on the core converting line (notebooks, cartons — [17_Factory_Operations_Plan.md](17_Factory_Operations_Plan.md) §17.2.2), not extrusion. Document the experience (employer/business name, role, dates) for the DPR.
* **Natural role split for Stage 1** ([07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.2): the two 18-year-olds run product/B2B sales/brand — energy-intensive, relationship-building, digitally-native work. Your father runs day-to-day factory-floor operations and vendor/landlord/local-official relationships, where age and presence carry practical weight (leasing, electricity/trade-licence clerks, equipment vendors).
* Not fixed — swap based on actual strengths — but don't leave the split undecided; ambiguity between three is worse than between two.

## 8.2 What the three of you own directly — don't outsource

* The feedstock supplier relationship(s) — this is your moat, keep it founder-owned.
* B2B sales for your first 5–10 anchor accounts — you need the direct customer feedback loop.
* The subsidy/loan application itself, even with a consultant drafting it — whoever signs (likely your father as guarantor, §8.0) needs to understand every clause personally.

## 8.3 Automation-first labour philosophy

Maximum output per person, no manual labour force, machines doing repetitive work, floor humans technically literate enough to operate/troubleshoot — not stand in an assembly line.

* **Buy the automated machine, not the cheaper manual one**, even at higher upfront cost ([07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.2.1). A ₹6–10L fully-automatic PLC converting/finishing line run by one trained operator replaces 3–5 manual-station workers.
* **Hire operators, not labourers.** Stage 1 headcount stays small — 2–4 people who can run/monitor/troubleshoot PLC-controlled equipment (ITI diploma or equivalent) — not a shift crew. Extrusion adds a second line and a real formulation-quality job, a higher bar than converting-only.
* **Scales the same way into Stage 2** — Stage 2-scale extrusion lines are inherently PLC/automated already ([03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md) §4.6.2), so the "few skilled operators, no manual crew" model carries forward.
* **Where automation genuinely isn't worth it** (e.g. final packing/pasting at Stage 1 scale), leave it manual — the point is not carrying a labour force where a machine plus one operator does the job.
