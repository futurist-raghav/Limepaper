# Core Team & Experts Needed

*Part of the Limepaper business plan — see [00_README_Index.md](00_README_Index.md) for the full document set.*

At three of you, you can't hire everyone up front. The sequencing below prioritises whichever expert unlocks the *next* bottleneck — capital, compliance, product, or revenue — not a wish-list.

## 8.0 Your existing team of three

The team isn't really "two founders" — it's three: two 18-year-old co-founders and one senior family member (father), the eldest and most experienced person on the team. Worth being deliberate about this rather than treating him as an informal helper:

* **Formally involve him in the entity from day one** (co-founder, director, or documented advisor with equity/stake — get CA/legal advice on the exact structure). This matters concretely later: when Stage 2 ([07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.3) requires a bank loan or PMEGP application, a co-applicant or guarantor with more credit/asset history than two 18-year-olds meaningfully strengthens the application. Setting this up now avoids scrambling to add him formally under time pressure later.
* **Natural role split for Stage 1** ([07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.2): the two 18-year-olds are well-placed to run product/B2B sales/brand — the energy-intensive, relationship-building, digitally-native work. Your father is well-placed to run day-to-day factory-floor operations and vendor/landlord/local-official relationships, where age and presence often carry practical weight in India's small-manufacturing/local-government context (leasing a unit, dealing with electricity/trade-licence clerks, negotiating with equipment vendors).
* This isn't a fixed assignment — swap based on actual strengths, not just age — but don't leave the split undecided; ambiguity between three people is worse than between two.

## 8.2 What the three of you should own directly — don't outsource

* The relationship with your feedstock supplier(s) — this is your moat; keep it founder-owned.

* B2B sales for your first 5–10 anchor accounts — you need the direct customer feedback loop.

* The subsidy/loan application process itself, even with a consultant drafting it — whoever signs (likely your father as guarantor, per §8.0) needs to understand every clause personally.

## 8.3 Automation-first labour philosophy

You've been explicit: maximum output per person, no "timepass" manual labour force, machines doing the repetitive work, and any humans on the floor being technically literate people who can operate/troubleshoot equipment — not stand in an assembly line. Concretely, that means:

* **Buy the automated machine, not the cheaper manual one**, even when it costs more upfront — see the equipment trade-off table in [07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.2.1. A ₹6–12 lakh fully-automatic PLC notebook-converting line run by one trained operator replaces what would otherwise be 3–5 manual-station workers.
* **Hire operators, not labourers.** The Stage 1 headcount you actually need is small: 1–2 people who can run, monitor and do basic troubleshooting on a PLC-controlled machine (an ITI diploma in mechanical/electrical trades, or equivalent hands-on experience, is the right bar) — not a shift crew.
* **This scales the same way into Stage 2.** Extrusion lines at the scale you'd buy are inherently PLC/automated already (§4.6.2) — the same "few skilled operators, no manual crew" model carries forward rather than needing to be rebuilt.
* **Where automation genuinely isn't available or worth it** (e.g. final packing/pasting at Stage 1 scale — [07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.2.1), that's fine to leave manual; the point isn't to automate everything reflexively, it's to not carry a labour force where a machine plus one operator does the job.
