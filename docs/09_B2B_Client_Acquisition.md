# B2B Client Acquisition Playbook

*Part of the Limepaper business plan — see [00_README_Index.md](00_README_Index.md). §11.0 added 31 Aug 2026 to scope this doc against the two-channel model in [03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md) §4.7.*

## 11.0 What this playbook covers, and what it doesn't

Two different customers buy the same extruded sheet, and this doc covers only one. **Entirely Channel 1: raw substrate — sheet, roll stock, private-label blanks — to converters and brands who finish it themselves.** Every target segment in §11.1 is a buyer of material, not a Limepaper-branded finished product.

**Channel 2 — finished, branded goods under our own name** (notebooks, cartons, tags, labels, carry bags — the ten SKUs in [18_Product_SKU_Catalog.md](18_Product_SKU_Catalog.md), converted in-house per [17_Factory_Operations_Plan.md](17_Factory_Operations_Plan.md) §17.2.2) is a separate motion with its own customer (retail/D2C, brand procurement) and mechanics (storefront, marketplace, brand story) — lives in [08_Implementation_Roadmap.md](08_Implementation_Roadmap.md) Phase F, not here. This doc's tactics (IndiaMART, trade shows, lab-data pitches) are built for a converter buying material, not a retail sale.

**In-house use** is neither — the plant consuming its own output before external sale, covered in [03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md) §4.7.

## 11.1 Who to target, in priority order

1. **Packaging converters/printers already serving FMCG/pharma brands** — sell them substrate; they convert it. Lower CAC than pitching brands directly.
2. **E-commerce mailer/label buyers** — high volume, standardised specs, good for proving consistent quality at scale.
3. **Corporate gifting and premium print houses** — smaller volume, higher margin, good early revenue while scaling.
4. **Industrial labels & tags converters** — fastest-growing sub-segment ([02_Market_Opportunity.md](02_Market_Opportunity.md) §3.2), high-margin, low-volume entry.
5. **Institutional/government** — school/college notebook tenders, PSU gifting programmes — price-sensitive but large, reliable volume once qualified as a vendor.

## 11.2 Tactics

* **Lead with lab data, not a pitch deck.** Procurement needs tear-strength, water-resistance and GSM-equivalent numbers before sampling. Get third-party lab certification early ([08_Implementation_Roadmap.md](08_Implementation_Roadmap.md) Phase B, step 11) and put it on one page.
* **List on IndiaMART/TradeIndia** — every competitor in [04_Competitive_Landscape.md](04_Competitive_Landscape.md) has a presence there; low-cost inbound lead gen where converters actually search.
* **Exhibit at India's packaging/print trade shows** (PackPlus, PrintPack India) — where converters and FMCG buyers source new substrates. Budget one show once samples exist.
* **Pre-sell before commissioning.** Map 3–5 target anchor customers, get signed LOIs **before the Stage 1 equipment order** ([07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.2.4, [08_Implementation_Roadmap.md](08_Implementation_Roadmap.md) step 17). With the converting-only first stage gone, ₹1.5cr of capex lands ahead of revenue — these LOIs gate that spend, not just a sales tactic.
* **Use the sustainability/ESG hook directly with brand procurement**, not just converters — FMCG packaging-sustainability scorecards reward "tree-free, water-free, made from diverted marble waste." Ask early contacts for their ESG/packaging-sustainability lead.
* **Turn the Ston2pack gap into a pitch.** If genuinely dormant ([04_Competitive_Landscape.md](04_Competitive_Landscape.md) §5.4), any converter/brand that sampled their 2021–23 product is a warm lead worth identifying.

## 11.3 Sequencing against the rest of the plan

Runs continuously from Week 6 of [08_Implementation_Roadmap.md](08_Implementation_Roadmap.md) (Phase E) — not a later-stage activity. The first 2–3 signed LOIs double as:

1. The gate on Stage 1 capex — no signed offtake, no equipment order ([07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.2.4).
2. Collateral for the Stage 1 term loan, and later the ₹15cr Stage 2 round (§9.3.2) — lenders and investors both want offtake proof first. Stage 2's bar is higher: repeat purchase history, not LOIs.
