# Vendor, Hiring & Capital Due Diligence — Protecting the Money, the Team, and the Decisions

*Part of the Limepaper business plan — see [00_README_Index.md](00_README_Index.md). This doc exists for one reason: at three of you, two of whom are 18, every vendor, consultant, contractor and candidate knows they're dealing with first-time buyers. This is the checklist that closes that gap. Pair with [11_Learning_Resources.md](11_Learning_Resources.md) — literacy and process discipline work together.*

## 12.1 Machinery vendor due diligence (Stage 1 and Stage 2 equipment)

Applies to every purchase — the ₹6–12L converting/finishing line and, at much higher stakes, the ₹70–95L extrusion line, **both now Stage 1 purchases** ([07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md)).

### Before you ever send money

* **Verify the company independently of the IndiaMART/TradeIndia listing**: GST registration, MCA company search, and an IEC if they claim to export/import — all free, checkable in minutes on government portals.
* **Search the company, directors, and phone number with "fraud," "complaint," "cheated," "scam."** Look for prior disputes, recovery notices, or matching patterns.
* **Confirm the factory address is real industrial premises**, not a residential flat or virtual office — satellite/street-view imagery plus a call to a landline, not just WhatsApp.
* **No visitable address, or "we only communicate on WhatsApp,"** is a serious red flag on its own.

### The site visit (never skip for the extrusion line; strongly prefer for everything else)

* Count machines against the quote; note whether they look in regular productive use or staged for your visit.
* Ask when key equipment was purchased and to see maintenance records — no log at all is worth questioning.
* Count floor workers against claimed production volumes — inconsistency is a real tell.
* Inspect raw-material storage — genuine production leaves visible stock.
* Check power backup and (if relevant) water treatment — claimed output needs matching utilities.
* Watch it actually run, ideally with your own material, not just a pre-recorded video.

### Red flags to walk away from

* Factory "under renovation" or the visit repeatedly rescheduled.
* Equipment present but visibly not operational, dusty, or not the model discussed.
* Workers who don't know basic specifics of what they're producing.
* Only photocopied certificates, no originals on-site.
* Factory address doesn't match GST/MCA registration.
* Any request for **100% payment in advance** — a genuine vendor almost always accepts staged payment.

### Payment structure — never pay it all upfront

* Standard structure: deposit to confirm order (20–30%), a tranche on pre-shipment inspection or factory acceptance test (FAT), final tranche only on successful site installation/commissioning (SAT). A vendor refusing any staging, especially for a first-time buyer, is itself a red flag.
* **For lakhs-to-crores purchases — the extrusion line qualifies in year one — get independent third-party inspection** before final payment (SGS, Bureau Veritas, Intertek, AsiaInspection — a few hundred to low-thousands of dollars, cheap insurance against the amount at risk).
* Put warranty, AMC terms, spares availability, and training commitments **in the written contract**, not verbal promises.

## 12.2 Feedstock supplier due diligence

Specific to marble slurry/CaCO3 suppliers, given the purity issues in [03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md) §4.2.1 and [05_Risks_Diligence.md](05_Risks_Diligence.md):

* **Lab-test every new source before signing, and re-test periodically after** — not just the first sample. Resistance to ongoing testing, or drifting quality post-contract, is a known commodity-sourcing failure pattern.
* **Get purity/whiteness/particle-size specs written into the supply agreement**, with a rejection/return clause for out-of-spec batches.
* **Prefer suppliers with a checkable track record** (other customers you can call) over the cheapest unknown quote, especially in your first 6–12 months while calibrating your own QC.

## 12.3 Hiring & team due diligence

Applies to every technical hire — process engineer, converting-line technician, QC specialist ([06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md)) — and any consultant or contractor.

* **Verify credentials directly**: call the institute for a diploma/ITI certificate, call a listed previous employer rather than trusting only a written reference.
* **Use a paid skills test or short trial task before a full hire**, especially machine-operator roles — claimed PLC experience should be demonstrable on the actual or a similar machine.
* **Never pay a candidate's "training/certification fee"** — a well-known pattern against inexperienced employers; training cost is yours to bear as employer.
* **Put everything in writing from day one:** offer letter with role/compensation/probation terms; an NDA and IP-assignment clause for anyone touching process knowledge or supplier relationships (your feedstock relationship is explicitly your moat — [06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) §8.2); a notice period.
* **For any consultant (DPR, compliance, legal, CA), confirm they're independently engaged by you**, not recommended-and-controlled by the vendor whose equipment or scheme they're advising on. A "free" DPR consultant who only recommends one machinery vendor isn't independent.

## 12.4 Capital & contract due diligence

* **Never sign a guarantee, loan document, or supplier contract without reading every clause yourself** — even with a CA or lawyer present, you (and your father, [06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) §8.0) are legally bound.
* **Use your own lawyer, not the counterparty's.** A vendor/landlord/investor offering to "handle the paperwork" or recommending "their guy" is a conflict of interest — get [06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) role #4 to review anything before signing, no exceptions.
* **Understand CGTMSE correctly:** the guarantee covers the *bank*, not you — it enables collateral-free lending, but you remain personally liable. Don't let "collateral-free" framing suggest the debt itself is lower-risk.
* **Hold the line on the ₹1.5 crore ceiling** ([07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.1) against anyone suggesting "just borrow a bit more." It's now backed by a real family asset (the ₹70L shop, §9.2.2) — more important to hold, not less. The two ring-fenced reserves inside it are not a cushion to go over.

## 12.5 A standing discipline, not a one-time checklist

* **Keep a shared running log** of every due-diligence check performed, who did it, and what was found — across three people, "someone probably checked that" is exactly how gaps happen.
* **Slow down when someone pushes urgency** ("this price is only good today," "sign now or lose the slot") — urgency pressure is one of the most consistent fraud tells, across vendor and hiring alike.
* **Revisit this checklist before every new category of spend** — extrusion and converting lines, feedstock contracts, Stage 2 machinery, first real hires, and again before any Stage 2 loan documents.
