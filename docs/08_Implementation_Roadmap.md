# Implementation Roadmap (Two-Founder Team)

*Part of the Limepaper business plan — see [00_README_Index.md](00_README_Index.md) for the full document set.*

Organised by phase, roughly timed, for your actual team of three. "F1/F2" = either 18-year-old co-founder — assign by strength. "P" = your father, the team's senior member — see [06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) §8.0 for why his role is formalised, not informal. This roadmap reflects the revised strategy in [07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md): build the minimum automated **converting factory** now, self-fund it, and only raise capital for backward integration into extrusion once Stage 1 has a real 12–24 month track record.

## Phase A — Foundation (Weeks 1–6)

1. Finalise the core idea one-pager in [01_Core_Idea_Pitch_Summary.md](01_Core_Idea_Pitch_Summary.md) — fill in founder bios (all three of you) and the initial ask placeholder.

2. Register the entity (LLP or Pvt Ltd — get CA advice; Pvt Ltd is usually better if you plan to raise equity later) with **all three of you formally on it** — see [06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) §8.0 for why this matters for Stage 2 loan applications.

3. Reserve and trademark-file the brand name.

4. Open a business bank account and set up basic bookkeeping (CA-led).

5. P: explore the silver-backed loan option ([07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.2.2) to convert part of your ₹10 lakh into working cash without selling the asset — an adult co-applicant often gets better terms.

6. F1: start supplier-side research (finished stone-paper roll/sheet suppliers — Indian and Chinese, since Stage 1 buys in rather than extrudes). F2: start customer-side research (packaging converters, notebook buyers, potential B2B leads). P: start scouting a small leasable unit and building local vendor/landlord relationships (§8.0).

## Phase B — Core Team & Research (Weeks 4–12, overlaps Phase A)

7. Skip a formal DPR consultant for now — Stage 1 doesn't need one ([06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) role #1 is deferred to Stage 2). Instead, one of you drafts a simple internal costing sheet covering equipment, stock, working capital against the [07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.2.1 budget.

8. Engage a compliance/EHS consultant (light touch) to map exactly which licences Stage 1 (converting only, no chemical process) actually requires — far lighter than a full factory: Udyam/MSME registration, GST, local trade licence; Pollution Control Board consent is **not** required yet since no extrusion/chemical process is running.

9. Run a competitive-intelligence pass: place trial orders or get samples from Karst, any China stone-paper suppliers, and — if reachable — Ston2pack directly ([04_Competitive_Landscape.md](04_Competitive_Landscape.md) §5.4) to benchmark real finished-product quality and pricing.

10. Get three firm quotes each for: (a) finished stone-paper roll/sheet stock at trial-batch volumes, (b) converting equipment (cutting/binding/printing), (c) lab testing for tear/waterproof certification.

11. Try to reach the Ston2pack founder or anyone who bought from them — cheap diligence on why the category's one branded India player went quiet ([04_Competitive_Landscape.md](04_Competitive_Landscape.md) §5.4).

## Phase C — Process & Compliance (Weeks 8–16)

12. File for all Phase-0-relevant registrations identified in step 8.

13. Lock a finished-material supply agreement (bulk stone-paper roll/sheet stock) — this is Stage 1's actual raw-material supply chain, and sourcing it well now also builds the supplier relationships you'll draw on for Stage 2's own feedstock (raw slurry/CaCO₃) later.

14. Set up basic QC: how you'll verify incoming stone-paper stock meets spec (thickness, tear strength, printability) before it enters your converting line.

15. Draft your B2B sample/spec sheet (tear resistance, waterproofness, GSM-equivalent weight, printability) — this is sales collateral, not just internal documentation. See [09_B2B_Client_Acquisition.md](09_B2B_Client_Acquisition.md).

## Phase D — The Real Factory: Automated Converting Unit (Weeks 10–20)

16. P leads: lease a small unit (500–1,500 sq ft is enough) — Kishangarh/Rajsamand for proximity to future feedstock and the marble-belt story, or a cheaper location if converting doesn't need to be co-located yet.

17. Order the **fully-automatic PLC notebook/converting machine** ([07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.2.1, ₹6–12 lakh) — not a manual multi-station setup. Outsource printing to a local printer initially rather than buying separate print equipment.

18. Hire **one, at most two, trained machine operators/technicians** (ITI/diploma-level, not general labour — [06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) §8.3) to run the line day-to-day; the three of you supervise/QC rather than staff the floor yourselves long-term.

19. Run a real production batch (not just a trial): convert purchased stone-paper stock into notebooks/packaging, QC against your spec sheet, and start selling immediately — this is production start, not a pilot to be repeated later.

## Phase E — B2B Pipeline (starts Week 6, runs continuously — full playbook in [09_B2B_Client_Acquisition.md](09_B2B_Client_Acquisition.md))

20. Build a target list of 20–30 B2B prospects across packaging converters, FMCG/pharma buyers, e-commerce label buyers.

21. Get your first 5 samples out the door with lab-test data attached.

22. Convert samples into at least 2–3 signed LOIs or pilot purchase orders — these are real Stage 1 revenue now, and they'll double as fundraising collateral whenever Stage 2 fundraising eventually starts.

## Phase F — B2C Setup (Weeks 16–26)

23. Launch a simple D2C storefront (Shopify/WooCommerce or an Instagram-led shop) for the Whitestone-style stationery line — "made from Kishangarh's marble waste" is your headline story.

24. List on Amazon/Flipkart and relevant marketplaces once you have consistent stock.

25. Use B2C as marketing for the B2B story, not as the primary revenue driver at this stage (per the sequencing logic in [04_Competitive_Landscape.md](04_Competitive_Landscape.md) §5.3).

## Phase G — Run Stage 1 for Real (Months 6–24, not a fixed date — gated by results)

This is the phase that actually matters most, and it isn't a quick step — it's most of your first two years. There's no calendar trigger here; you move to Stage 2 when the numbers say so, not when a clock runs out.

26. Run the factory, sell continuously, reinvest profit into working capital and (once justified by demand) a second machine or shift, per the operating philosophy in [07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.2.3.

27. Track cost-per-unit, margin per SKU, and machine utilisation from day one — this is the data that makes Stage 2 fundable later without reconstructing it from scratch.

28. Target: recover your ₹10–15 lakh setup cost within 12 months of steady production; use months 12–24 to build a real profit history and 3–5 anchor B2B relationships.

29. Once you have that track record: engage the DPR consultant and compliance/EHS consultant properly for the first time ([06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) roles #1, #3), begin the PMEGP/CGTMSE application (P as co-applicant/guarantor strengthens it), and shortlist/site-visit 2–3 automated extrusion-line vendors (role #7) — do not buy off a single listing without a site visit or verified reference customer.

30. On funding close: order the pilot extrusion line, hire the process engineer (role #5) full-time, and start the compliance process for full manufacturing consent (Pollution Control Board Consent to Establish/Operate — now required since you're running an extrusion process). First captive-manufactured stone paper roll off the line = Stage 2 production start, backward-integrating on top of the Stage 1 business that's already running. **Total borrowed capital across Stage 1 + Stage 2 stays at or under ₹1.5 crore ([07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.1) — no exceptions.**

31. Once Stage 2 is running: track loan repayment specifically, not just general profitability. **Do not discuss, plan, or approach anyone about further capital until at least 50% of the Stage 2 loan principal is repaid from operating cash flow** ([07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.4) — that milestone, not a calendar date, is what opens the conversation about expansion.

## Quick checklist view

- [ ] Phase A — Foundation (entity with all 3 formally on it, trademark, bank account, silver loan explored)
- [ ] Phase B — Core team engaged (light touch), competitive intel gathered, quotes in hand
- [ ] Phase C — Registrations filed, feedstock agreement locked, spec sheet drafted
- [ ] Phase D — Automated converting unit leased, equipped, staffed with 1–2 technicians, real production run started
- [ ] Phase E — B2B pipeline built, samples out, 2–3 LOIs signed
- [ ] Phase F — D2C storefront live, marketplace listings up
- [ ] Phase G — Stage 1 self-funded and profitable for 12–24 months → Stage 2 (extrusion) fundraising begins on that track record, capped at ₹1.5 crore total → no further capital raised until ≥50% of that loan is repaid from operations
