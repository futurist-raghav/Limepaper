# Key Risks & Diligence Items

*Part of the Limepaper business plan — see [00_README_Index.md](00_README_Index.md) for the full document set.*

* **Feedstock purity:** raw marble slurry composition varies by source (mix of marble/granite dust, possible contaminants), and cement-plant-adjacent CaCO3 sources add a different, often less consistent contaminant profile (kiln dust, silica) — confirm purity, whiteness index and iron content with a lab test before signing any offtake agreement; food/print-grade stone paper needs consistently high purity, which raw slurry alone does not guarantee without a real purification step. This is now priced explicitly (not assumed away) in [03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md) §4.2.1 and [07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.3.1. Owner: mineral processing/QC specialist, [06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) role #6.

* **Capital discipline:** the plan runs on a hard ₹1.5 crore ceiling across Stage 1 + Stage 2 ([07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.1) — the real risk isn't the ceiling itself, it's the temptation to breach it once real vendor quotes come in higher than the illustrative numbers. If a firm quote makes a tier unaffordable, the correct response is a smaller/used machine or a deferred purchase, not a higher loan.

* **HDPE price exposure:** since HDPE is the largest cost line (see [03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md) §4.6.1) and is not a local input, factor in petrochemical price volatility and consider hedging or long-term supply contracts with domestic producers.

* **Regulatory/consent:** marble slurry handling is under CPCB/NGT scrutiny in Rajasthan — using it as a raw material is generally viewed favourably (waste-to-value), but plant consents (State Pollution Control Board) and transport permissions still need to be secured. Owner: compliance/EHS consultant, [06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) role #3.

* **Market education cost:** stone paper commands a price premium over commodity paper — budget for sales cycles that require sampling, technical proof (tear/waterproof tests) and sustainability documentation for procurement teams. See [09_B2B_Client_Acquisition.md](09_B2B_Client_Acquisition.md).

* **Data quality:** several figures in this document set come from secondary market-research vendors and one project-report house (EIRI); treat them as planning inputs, not audited numbers, and validate the big-ticket ones (capex, HDPE landed cost, offtake pricing) directly with suppliers and customers before committing capital.

* **Competitor precedent:** India's only branded stone-paper company (Ston2pack) appears to have gone dormant after 2–3 years — see [04_Competitive_Landscape.md](04_Competitive_Landscape.md) §5.4 for the full read and a cheap diligence step (find out why) before committing capital.

* **GST/HSN classification is unresolved — a real, unquantified margin risk:** stone paper is a CaCO3/HDPE composite, not pulp-based paper, so it doesn't sit cleanly in any existing GST category. It could plausibly be classified under Chapter 48 (paper, 5–12% GST) or HSN 3921 (plastic sheet/film, 18% GST) or even Chapter 68 (articles of stone) — no public precedent or ruling was found either way ([13_Paper_Industry_Economics_Deep_Dive.md](13_Paper_Industry_Economics_Deep_Dive.md) §13.6). A 5–12% vs. 18% swing is a material difference in both output pricing competitiveness and input-tax-credit eligibility. **This is now the single highest-priority open regulatory question in the whole plan — get a formal GST advance ruling before finalising Stage 2 pricing or capital commitment**, not something to discover after the plant is running. Owner: compliance/EHS consultant or a GST practitioner, [06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) role #3.

* **The "40% lower capex" claim doesn't hold against India's real furnish mix:** earlier drafts of this plan ([03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md) §4.6.2) cited stone paper as ~40% cheaper to build per tonne of capacity than "a conventional paper mill" — sourced to a generic global cost-model article comparing stone paper to virgin wood-pulp mills. Real Indian capex data ([13_Paper_Industry_Economics_Deep_Dive.md](13_Paper_Industry_Economics_Deep_Dive.md) §13.3.3) shows recycled-fibre paper/board capacity — which is what ~74% of India's industry actually runs on — expands at roughly ₹36,000–91,000/tonne, well below stone paper's own ≈₹121,000–123,000/tonne benchmarks. Don't repeat the "cheaper capex" claim to lenders/investors without qualifying it as "vs. virgin wood-pulp mills only" — the real, defensible advantages are water and energy footprint, not capex.

## Recommended Next Steps (quick list)

*Superseded in detail by the full phase-by-phase plan in [08_Implementation_Roadmap.md](08_Implementation_Roadmap.md), but kept here as the original condensed version:*

* Commission a formal techno-economic feasibility study (EIRI or a bank-empanelled chartered engineer) sized to your target capacity, once you've shortlisted a plot in Kishangarh or Rajsamand.

* Get 2–3 firm raw-material quotes: (a) marble slurry/CaCO3 powder at stone-paper-grade fineness from local millers, and (b) HDPE resin from a domestic petrochemical distributor.

* Engage Rajasthan's Department of Industries / RIICO early on RIPS 2024 and the Circular Economy Incentive Scheme 2025 — several benefits are capped to the "first 5" or "first 20" approved units.

* Order small trial batches or a pilot line to validate purity/whiteness of local slurry against stone-paper specifications before scaling capex commitments.

* Map 3–5 target B2B anchor customers (FMCG/pharma packaging converters, e-commerce label buyers) to pre-sell initial capacity before the plant is commissioned.
