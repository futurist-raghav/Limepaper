# Key Risks & Diligence Items

*Part of the Limepaper business plan — see [00_README_Index.md](00_README_Index.md).*

* **Feedstock purity:** raw marble slurry varies by source, and cement-plant-adjacent CaCO3 adds a different, often less consistent contaminant profile (kiln dust, silica) — confirm purity, whiteness index and iron content by lab test before any offtake agreement. Priced explicitly in [03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md) §4.2.1 and [07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.2.3. Owner: mineral processing/QC specialist ([06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) role #6).

* **Capital discipline:** Stage 1 runs on a hard ₹1.5 crore borrowing ceiling ([07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.1). The real risk is breaching it once firm quotes come in higher than illustrative numbers — correct response is a smaller/used machine or deferred purchase, not a bigger loan. The two ring-fenced reserves inside the ceiling (₹10L salary/daily-expense, ₹8L emergency — §9.2.1) exist to stay untouched by ordinary cost pressure.

* **Capex ahead of revenue — a risk this plan deliberately took on:** the earlier structure proved demand on a ₹10–15L converting setup before extrusion. That stage was removed 23 Aug 2026, so collateral-backed debt is now committed before any sale. A brief push to ₹3 crore was withdrawn the same day as unfinanceable — ₹1.5 crore is the number this risk applies against. Mitigations are not optional: 3–5 signed LOIs before the equipment order ([08_Implementation_Roadmap.md](08_Implementation_Roadmap.md) step 17), a paid process engineer on formulation from Phase B, and vendor site-visit diligence on every machine. See [14_Founder_Reality_Check.md](14_Founder_Reality_Check.md) §6.

* **Promoter margin at ₹1.5 crore — a closeable gap, not an open risk:** banks typically want 20–25% promoter margin (≈₹30–37.5L) on a loan this size. Against the ₹70L shop plus ₹10L silver (≈₹80L hard collateral, [07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.2.2), that bar is comfortably covered — very different from the ₹3cr math, where the ₹60–75L bar vastly exceeded the ₹10L of silver that was the only quantified asset then. Still get the shop formally valued and confirm the bank's actual LTV before treating this as settled.

* **HDPE price exposure:** HDPE is the largest cost line ([03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md) §4.6.1) and not a local input — factor in petrochemical volatility, consider hedging or long-term domestic supply contracts.

* **Regulatory/consent:** marble slurry handling is under CPCB/NGT scrutiny; using it as raw material is generally viewed favourably (waste-to-value), but State Pollution Control Board consents and transport permissions still need securing. Owner: compliance/EHS consultant ([06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) role #3).

* **Market education cost:** stone paper commands a price premium over commodity paper — budget for sales cycles requiring sampling, tear/waterproof proof tests, and sustainability documentation. See [09_B2B_Client_Acquisition.md](09_B2B_Client_Acquisition.md).

* **Data quality:** many figures here come from secondary market-research vendors and one project-report house (EIRI) — planning inputs, not audited numbers. Validate the big-ticket ones (capex, HDPE landed cost, offtake pricing) directly with suppliers/customers before committing capital.

* **Competitor precedent:** India's only branded stone-paper company (Ston2pack) appears dormant after 2–3 years — [04_Competitive_Landscape.md](04_Competitive_Landscape.md) §5.4 has the full read and a cheap diligence step (find out why) before committing capital.

* **GST/HSN classification unresolved — a real, unquantified margin risk:** stone paper (CaCO3/HDPE composite) doesn't sit cleanly in any GST category — could plausibly be Chapter 48 (paper, 5–12%), HSN 3921 (plastic sheet/film, 18%), or Chapter 68 (articles of stone); no public precedent found either way ([13_Paper_Industry_Economics_Deep_Dive.md](13_Paper_Industry_Economics_Deep_Dive.md) §13.6). A 5–12% vs. 18% swing materially affects both pricing competitiveness and input-tax-credit eligibility. **Highest-priority open regulatory question in the plan — get a formal GST advance ruling before finalising pricing or capital commitment.** Owner: compliance/EHS consultant or GST practitioner ([06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) role #3).

* **The "40% lower capex" claim doesn't hold against India's real furnish mix:** [03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md) §4.6.2 cites stone paper as ~40% cheaper to build per tonne than "a conventional paper mill," sourced to a generic global comparison against virgin wood-pulp mills. Real Indian data ([13_Paper_Industry_Economics_Deep_Dive.md](13_Paper_Industry_Economics_Deep_Dive.md) §13.3.3) shows recycled-fibre capacity — ~74% of India's industry — expands at ≈₹36,000–91,000/tonne, well below stone paper's own ≈₹121,000–123,000/tonne. Don't repeat "cheaper capex" to lenders/investors without qualifying "vs. virgin wood-pulp mills only" — the defensible advantages are water and energy footprint, not capex.

## Recommended Next Steps (quick list)

*Superseded in detail by [08_Implementation_Roadmap.md](08_Implementation_Roadmap.md); kept here as the original condensed version.*

* Commission a formal techno-economic feasibility study (EIRI or a bank-empanelled chartered engineer) once a plot in Kishangarh/Rajsamand is shortlisted.
* Get 2–3 firm raw-material quotes: stone-paper-grade CaCO3 powder from local millers, and HDPE resin from a domestic petrochemical distributor.
* Engage Rajasthan's Department of Industries/RIICO early on RIPS 2024 and the Circular Economy Incentive Scheme 2025 — several benefits cap at "first 5"/"first 20" approved units.
* Trial batches or a pilot line to validate purity/whiteness of local slurry before scaling capex.
* Map 3–5 target B2B anchor customers (FMCG/pharma packaging converters, e-commerce label buyers) to pre-sell capacity before commissioning.
