# Learning Resources — Printing, Papermaking, Extrusion & Converting

*Part of the Limepaper business plan — see [00_README_Index.md](00_README_Index.md). ## How to use this doc

Study in order — each module builds vocabulary the next assumes. You don't need to become an engineer; you need enough depth to tell whether "our extruder runs a 32:1 L/D ratio screw" or "this needs UV-flexo, not offset" is a normal statement or a red flag. Module A and C matter most before Stage 1 purchases ([07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.2); C, E and F matter most before Stage 2 (§9.3).

## Module A — Printing Fundamentals (before any equipment purchase)

Stone paper doesn't print like wood-pulp paper — get this wrong and your first batch comes out with ink that smudges, doesn't cure, or won't adhere.

* **Core fact:** stone paper is a non-porous, coated plastic-mineral composite, not an absorbent fibre sheet. Offset relies on ink absorbing into a porous surface — stone paper's non-absorbency works against it. Flexographic printing with **UV-curable ink** (cured by light, not absorption/evaporation) is the better-suited process. Confirm directly with your printer/ink supplier before any print run.
* **Structured course:** the **Indian Institute of Packaging (IIP)**, an autonomous body under the Ministry of Commerce & Industry, runs a **3-month full-time Certificate Course in Packaging Technology** (10+2 minimum) covering lithography, gravure, and silkscreen alongside packaging materials and testing. Centres in Mumbai (flagship), Delhi, Chennai, Hyderabad, Kolkata, Ahmedabad — the most efficient path from zero to literate in one quarter, worth considering for whoever leads B2B/product. (iip-in.com)
* **Concepts to know by name:** offset vs. flexo vs. gravure vs. screen vs. digital — strengths, cost-per-unit at your volumes, substrate fit; UV-curable vs. solvent-based vs. water-based ink; GSM and its stone-paper-equivalent thickness measure; bleed, die-cut, registration, colour separation (CMYK).
* **Free reading:** Luminite's and FocusLabel's flexo-vs-offset comparison guides — fast vocabulary before your first vendor call.

## Module B — Papermaking & Stone-Paper Material Science

* Wikipedia "Stone paper" — solid, well-cited overview of composition/manufacturing/properties; your map before going deeper.
* Two Sides' "Stone Paper (mineral paper)" briefing (twosides.info) — independent, fact-checks marketing claims.
* "Stone Paper, an Eco-Friendly and Free-Tree Paper" (AIP Conference Proceedings) — short, technical.
* Patents worth skimming for background/summary sections: CN102673061A (food-grade stone paper), US5364610 (precipitated CaCO3 production), US6540878 (stabilized filler production).
* TAPPI — conventional wood-pulp papermaking terminology; the vocabulary your B2B customers and any paper-industry hire will already speak.

## Module C — Polymer Extrusion & Compounding (study seriously — year-one knowledge now)

The highest-value module now that extrusion sits in Stage 1 ([07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.2) — vendor claims are where a technically illiterate buyer is most exposed, and the line is your largest cheque.

* **Free course:** NPTEL/Swayam "Processing of Polymers and Polymer Composites" (IIT Roorkee, Dr. Inderdeep Singh) — 8 weeks, covers extrusion, injection moulding, thermoforming, compression/transfer moulding, thermoplastics materials science. Free; paid proctored exam for a certificate. A real engineering-school course — start here.
* **Reference books** (all well-regarded):
  * *Plastics Extrusion Technology Handbook* — Sidney Levy. Practical, technology-first — best fit for a founder.
  * *Extrusion: The Definitive Processing Guide and Handbook* — Giles, Wagner, Mount. For operators/engineers/managers; troubleshooting section plus compounding/blown-film/coextrusion case studies — directly relevant to your Stage 2 process.
  * *The Complete Technology Book on Plastic Extrusion, Moulding and Mould Design* — NIIR Board. India-specific, practical, useful for the small/mid-scale Indian machinery market.
* **Concepts to discuss confidently before signing anything:** screw L/D and compression ratio, single- vs. twin-screw compounding, die design (flat-die vs. blown-film), temperature-zone profiles, calendering/cooling-roll setup, throughput (kg/h) vs. line speed vs. sheet width.

## Module D — Converting Machinery & Automation

Relevant to the automated converting/finishing line downstream of your extruder ([07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.2.1) and the automation-first hiring philosophy ([06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) §8.3).

* **What to learn:** PLC basics — what it controls, reading a ladder-logic/HMI screen, "servo-driven" vs. "pneumatic" for precision and maintenance cost. You don't need to program one; you need to not be baffled by the term.
* **How to study cheaply:** Coursera/Udemy "PLC basics for beginners" — a few hours, sufficient for a buyer. A weekend, not a semester.
* **Direct and specific:** before buying, watch full demo videos from your shortlisted vendors (search company name + "notebook making machine"/"extrusion line" on YouTube). Compare against what's shown running in person — a machine that only exists in photos, never video, is itself a signal ([12_Vendor_Hiring_Due_Diligence.md](12_Vendor_Hiring_Due_Diligence.md)).

## Module E — Mineral Processing / Beneficiation

Relevant from Stage 1, since you now buy and qualify your own micronized CaCO3 rather than finished sheet ([03_Unit_Economics.md](03_Unit_Economics.md) §4.2.1) — critical before Stage 2 brings micronizing in-house ([07_Capital_Plan.md](07_Capital_Plan.md) §9.3.1).

* **Concepts:** micronization (grinding to target mesh/microns), whiteness index and why it matters for print quality, iron content's effect on discolouration, wet vs. dry grinding.
* **Best method:** a site visit to a local Kishangarh/Rajsamand micronizing unit with your mineral-processing QC consultant ([06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) role #6) teaches more in an afternoon than any course — prioritise it alongside reading.

## Module F — Compliance & Standards

* **BIS:** search bis.gov.in for relevant Indian Standards on paper/packaging your B2B buyers may require.
* **Pollution Control Board process:** Rajasthan PCB's website documents Consent to Establish/Operate directly — read the real process once, approaching Stage 2, rather than relying only on your consultant's summary.
* **Udyam/GST/MSME:** navigate udyamregistration.gov.in and gst.gov.in yourself once, even though your CA files the paperwork, so you know what your own entity's filings say.

## Module G — Business & Financial Literacy

* **Reading a DPR:** learn the standard sections (promoter background, market analysis, technical feasibility, cost of project, means of finance, profitability projections, break-even) so you can sanity-check a consultant's assumptions, not just trust the summary.
* **Loan mechanics to know cold:** term loan vs. working-capital loan; what a CGTMSE guarantee actually covers (it guarantees the *bank*, not you); what "collateral-free" really means; how co-applicant/guarantor liability works (relevant to your father's role — [06_Core_Team_Experts.md](06_Core_Team_Experts.md) §8.0).
