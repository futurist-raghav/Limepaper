## 5.2 India players

| Company | Model | Notes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |

*No India competitor identified currently advertises a captive, mine-mouth position inside the Rajasthan marble belt. That is a genuine differentiation angle: "we solve a local pollution problem and turn it into India's most credibly-sourced stone paper" is a story none of the above can tell as convincingly.*

## 5.3 B2B vs B2C — how to sequence go-to-market

* B2B first is the more common playbook (packaging leads the category at ~38–50% share) — target FMCG/pharma converters, e-commerce mailer/label buyers, and corporate-gifting/print houses. This absorbs volume fastest and validates unit economics with fewer SKUs.

* B2C stationery (notebooks, diaries, planners) is lower-volume but higher-margin and brand-building — Ston2pack's Whitestone and global Karst both use it this way: a visible consumer brand that also markets the B2B packaging business's sustainability credentials.

* A sensible sequence for a new Rajasthan entrant: launch with 1–2 B2B packaging/label SKUs to build cash flow and prove the plant, then layer in a D2C stationery line (notebooks/planners) that doubles as a marketing vehicle — "made from the marble waste of Kishangarh" is a genuinely strong consumer story, especially for corporate gifting and premium retail. Full B2C setup steps in [08_Implementation_Roadmap.md](08_Implementation_Roadmap.md) Phase F.

## 5.4 Reading the Ston2pack Signal — Opportunity or Warning?

Ston2pack (founded ~2021 by Mayur Umbratkar, a printing & packaging engineering graduate) is, as far as this research can find, the only India company that built a *branded* stone-paper business spanning both B2B packaging and B2C stationery (Whitestone Stationeries). It got real trade press (PrintWeek India) and LinkedIn traction through 2022.

Every signal found in this pass points the same way: the company website's copyright notice is frozen at "© 2021," its product photos carry 2020–21 file timestamps, and no press coverage or LinkedIn activity for the company or its founder turned up for 2023–2026 — consistent with what you've heard directly: active 2021–2023, no traces since. The LLP itself still shows as registered (Thane, Maharashtra) on company-search sites, which is not unusual for a stalled entity that was never formally wound up.

Two readings, and it's worth planning for both being partly true rather than picking one:

* **Opportunity read:** the one company that proved India demand exists for stone paper appears to have stalled — the market white space identified in §5.3 may be more open than it looks, and there may be dormant relationships worth reaching (converters or FMCG buyers who sampled Ston2pack's product, or ex-employees with hands-on process knowledge).

* **Warning read:** there is now a real, recent India example of a stone-paper venture stalling after 2–3 years. Before finalising plans, it's worth understanding *why* — HDPE/input cost volatility, working-capital strain, inability to hold consistent quality at scale, or a founder simply moving on are all plausible, and each implies a different mitigation for you.

**Recommended diligence (cheap, worth doing early):** try to reach the founder directly or find anyone who bought from Ston2pack; check the LLP's MCA/ROC filing status for signs of financial distress or formal closure; and note that if Ston2pack is truly inactive, any brand or converter that sampled their product in 2021–23 is a warm lead with existing category awareness (see [09_B2B_Client_Acquisition.md](09_B2B_Client_Acquisition.md) §11.2). This diligence step is also queued in [08_Implementation_Roadmap.md](08_Implementation_Roadmap.md) Phase B, step 11.
