The filter

Five gates. A product has to clear at least three.

Stone paper can physically become almost any paper product. That is exactly why a product list is worthless without a filter — the interesting question is never "can we make it," it is "does making it beat the thing it replaces."

Two binder variants

The same line runs a compostable version.

The 18% binder is the entire end-of-life problem, and it is also the one input we can swap without touching the machine. PBAT — polybutylene adipate-terephthalate — is compostable-certified, sold in film grade in Jaipur today, and runs on the same twin-screw line after a screw-temperature re-tune. It costs about 12–15% more on the binder line, not 130% more.

Variant A · standard
HDPE
₹100–115/kg, film grade
  • Commodity resin, deep domestic stock, no supply risk
  • Best stiffness and moisture barrier — the crisp, paper-like hand-feel is this
  • Not biodegradable. UV-photodegrades over 14–18 months into calcium carbonate plus microplastic fragments
  • Right for: signage, mailers, industrial labels, anything durable-by-design where end-of-life is years away
Variant B · compostable binder
PBAT
₹128–130/kg film grade — a 12–15% binder premium
  • IS 17088 / EN 13432 / OK Compost certified — India's own standard, not just an EU mark
  • Film-grade supply exists in Jaipur, local to the plant, plus deeper Gujarat supply
  • Honest caveat: certified for industrial composting — ~58°C, controlled microbes. It is not "degrades anywhere"
  • Softer than HDPE, closer to LDPE — needs a trial batch to confirm the hand-feel at our filler loading
  • Right for: gifting notebooks, hang tags, food-adjacent packaging — anywhere the buyer's ESG team reads the spec sheet

For completeness

The seven we are not building.

A product list that only contains winners is a marketing document. These failed the filter for specific, checkable reasons — and knowing why is what stops us wandering into them later.

Ruled out, and why

Loose A4 / A3 copier paper — at matched stiffness we are 2–3× the cost of wood-pulp at our own production cost, and 4–7× at market price. There is no version of this we win.
Marketplace e-commerce mailers — poly mailers run ₹1.30–6 a piece and are structurally 30–50% cheaper than any paper alternative. Amazon and Meesho scale is a price fight, not a product fight.
Rigid / set-up gift boxes — stiffness scales with the cube of thickness, so a 1.5mm greyboard core is out of reach of a 0.4mm sheet. We cannot be the structure. Supplying the printed wrap and liner over someone else's core is arithmetically attractive and worth a look once the Stage 1 line is proven — it is not a product we can quote today.
Corrugated fluting — the flute has to be creased into a wave without cracking. Our polymer content resists exactly that. We can supply the printed liner facing; we cannot be the core.
Direct-contact food wraps — FSSAI food-contact certification for the HDPE fraction is an unverified gate. Not a formality, and not something to discover after taking an order.
Mass-market gravure wallpaper — the volume segment prints on rotogravure with water-based ink, which our surface will not absorb. We fit the digital UV/latex custom-print niche instead, and only that.
PVC ID / access cards — a mature, very cheap, very thin incumbent. Our card-grade stiffness at that gauge is unverified and the displacement case is the weakest in the catalog.
